## What the PX4 logs show

- **Hover is steady.** Before the first pulse the heading moves ±0.1° (peak-to-peak 0.25–0.41°, yaw rate under 1 °/s) in all three runs. The ring is started by a turn; it is not a constant wobble.
- **PX4 does not hold a heading in this mode.** On the sim's command route (velocity + yaw rate through MAVROS), PX4's heading setpoint follows the actual heading sample by sample (error ≈ 0°). The only target is "yaw rate 0". So `MC_YAW_P` (heading hold) has no effect here: sets 1 and 2 differ only in it and give the same result.
- **The ring is the yaw-rate loop.** After release the yaw rate swings ±65 °/s at about 1 Hz (vehicle time) while its setpoint is 0. The swing decays slowly: about 18° peak-to-peak at 2 s, still about 5° at 15 s.
- **Damping follows P and I as theory says.** For a rate loop on a body with no friction, damping grows with P and falls with √I. Halving both lowers damping by about 0.7×, and set 1 got worse (24.6°). The 0.7× set sits between. So the sim's loop needs more damping (higher P, lower I), not less gain. The 2026-10-20 pitch fix (gains × 0.5) was the opposite case.

## Why the sim drone turns weakly (Pegasus model)

A quadcopter turns only through propeller drag: speeding up one diagonal pair and slowing the other leaves a net twist. In the sim (`thrusters/quadratic_thrust_curve.py`, `vehicles/multirotor.py`):

| Effect | Sim | Real drone |
|---|---|---|
| Twist per newton of thrust | `k_m / k_f` = 5.39e-8 / 3.96e-6 = **0.0136** N·m/N (CMU sys-ID) | PX4 `CA_ROTOR_KM` **0.05** — also PX4's default, possibly never measured on the Starling |
| Twist from motors speeding up / slowing down (rotor inertia) | not modelled | present: a fast kick that helps brake a turn |
| Air drag on rotation | none (`LinearDrag [0.5, 0.3, 0.0]` acts on velocity only) | some natural rotational damping |
| Motor lag | off (sim-only, 2026-10-20) | about 72 ms |

So the controller asks for a correction and the sim body answers with about a quarter of the twist the gains assume, with nothing else damping the swing. Open question for the real drone: if CMU's 0.0136 is right, the real Starling's yaw may also be weaker than its parameter assumes.
