# Align per-drone VIO frames; defer shared maps

**Bottom line.** For a Starling 2 Max swarm flying short indoor missions, the cheapest route to a consistent inter-drone frame is Family C: keep one VIO per drone and estimate a 4-DoF (x, y, z, yaw) correction between each drone's VIO frame and a common swarm frame from shared positions plus Nooploop UWB ranges, with an MRS-style estimator bank so the controller never sees a jump. No open-source package does this on ROS 2 Jazzy today, so the lab writes the estimator; the reference design is HKUST's Omni-swarm, whose paper reports **6–7 cm relative position and 1.3–4.3° relative rotation error between two drones** ([Omni-swarm, T-RO 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04131)). The catch, from the same paper, is that most of that accuracy comes from inter-drone visual loop closures, not UWB: its UWB-plus-odometry-only predecessor sat at **0.19–0.96 m** and **failed to initialise in parallel flight (0 % success)**, because relative yaw is unobservable from ranges alone without motion diversity. Family A (collaborative SLAM) is not worth adopting now: none of Swarm-SLAM, D²SLAM, Kimera-Multi or COVINS has been validated on drones with Jetson and RealSense under ROS 2; only Swarm-SLAM is ROS 2 and still maintained, and the one paper that quantifies the gain over plain VIO (Kimera-Multi) does so on 500–860 m ground-robot runs, a regime far from a hangar flight. Family B (localise in a pre-built map) has no published relocalisation success rate for cuVSLAM and a crash-prone, community-patched save/load path in ORB-SLAM3, so it is a one-day trial at most. Costs are engineering weeks rather than money: Family C needs an estimator, a yaw-initialisation procedure and a UWB calibration routine; Family A needs a multi-week port and CUDA models on Orin; Family B needs a JetPack 7.2 reflash for Jazzy-era Isaac ROS and still may not work. The single most useful thing to do before the November review is to measure the lab's own VIO drift against OptiTrack over a typical mission, then run the alignment estimator on the MILUV dataset (three D435i drones with inter-drone UWB and Vicon truth) and in a drift simulator built from the parameters tabulated below.

**How to read the numbers.** Every figure is tagged with where it came from: **[P]** a paper, **[R]** a repository, GitHub API, Docker Hub or dataset record, **[D]** a vendor datasheet or product documentation, **[F]** a forum or issue thread, **[derived]** arithmetic done on cited figures. Nothing here is a measurement by this lab. Items that could not be confirmed from a primary source are marked **UNVERIFIED**. Repository statistics were read on 2026-10-22.

## Collaborative SLAM has never been flown on a Jetson drone under ROS 2

The four Family A systems share one problem: none was validated in the lab's regime. Swarm-SLAM's only live run used three ground robots over 475 m with no ground truth, so the paper reports computation and communication statistics but no real-world accuracy ([Swarm-SLAM paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06230v3)). Kimera-Multi has by far the strongest field record, **eight Jackals over 7.8 km with up to 45 % WiFi packet drop**, but it is ROS 1, its distributed back-end took 100–174 s to converge, and its quantified benefit (VIO drift of 15–49 m cut to centimetres) belongs to runs twenty times longer than a hangar sortie ([field paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04362); [T-RO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386)). COVINS and COVINS-G have the best EuRoC numbers but all evaluation is replay, the repository has not been committed to since May 2023, and a third-party benchmark found "numerous failures" ([CSE dataset](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14775v1)). D²SLAM is the only one actually flown with a D435i, and its Orin Docker image exists, but it is ROS 1 Noetic, has no UWB, and its last commit is a refactor left "modified half way through" ([D²SLAM repo](https://github.com/HKUST-Aerial-Robotics/D2SLAM)). The platform constraint cuts the other way too: JetPack 6.x is Ubuntu 22.04, so Jazzy on Orin needs either a container or the JetPack 7.2 path NVIDIA confirmed in August 2026 ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/roadmap-for-isaac-ros-jazzy-on-orin-devices/375982)).

### Short notes per method

**Swarm-SLAM.** Sparse decentralised back-end (pose graph + Graduated Non-Convexity) that takes odometry from any ROS 2 topic; RTAB-Map in the paper. Two-stage loop closure keeps back-end traffic to 51–485 kB per dataset run [P]. Authors recommend Zenoh DDS bridging with one `ROS_DOMAIN_ID` per robot ([startup docs](https://lajoiepy.github.io/cslam_documentation/html/md_startup_instructions.html)). Weaknesses for the lab: never on a drone, no real-world ATE, CosPlace needs Torch on Orin (undocumented there), degrades in dynamic scenes (13.7 m ATE in one CSE hospital run) [P]. Build failures appear in issues #32, #41, #45, #59 [R]. Cost estimate (ours, not a paper figure): 1–2 weeks to a two-drone Docker spike with mocap truth.

**D²SLAM.** Decentralised VIO (D²VINS) plus distributed PGO with near/far modes; near mode fires at 3–5 m predicted separation and "needs a robust, low-latency network" [P]. Real D435i flights gave 5–7 cm per-axis relative error without yaw motion, roughly doubling with yaw, which the authors attribute to limited stereo field-of-view overlap ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.01538v4)). SuperPoint about 15 ms per image on Orin per a user; SuperGlue "EXTREME SLOW" per README [F/R]. A two-Jetson-NX user saw the other drone's PGO only after fixing multicast and found it stuttering ([issue #39](https://github.com/HKUST-Aerial-Robotics/D2SLAM/issues/39)). No UWB factor exists. Cost (ours): days to run the container on one Orin; a ROS 2 port plus UWB factor would be new engineering of unknown size.

**Kimera-Multi.** Fully distributed; DBoW2 place recognition, ORB keypoints for geometric verification, D-GNC on RBCD for PGO [P]. On the Stata runs the default solver left one robot 33 m off; fixing it took 2000 iterations (14 min) [P Table V]. Field lessons: BoW "detects too many matches indoor and not enough outdoor"; opposite-direction revisits produce no loop closure [P]. Requires Kimera-VIO; the multi-master transport is unreleased; no licence file on the index repo [R]. Its D455 campus datasets are request-only via Google Form ([Kimera-Multi-Data](https://github.com/MIT-SPARK/Kimera-Multi-Data)). Use it as the citation for "when loop closures matter", not as software.

**COVINS / COVINS-G.** Centralised: agents run VIO, a server does place recognition, PGO and (COVINS only) global bundle adjustment; agent→server 400–600 kB/s in COVINS, ~180 kB/s in COVINS-G; server→agent a 2 Hz drift-corrected pose [P]. COVINS-G accepts heterogeneous front-ends in one mission (0.081–0.090 m RMSE on EuRoC with five different VIOs) [P]. Issues #55, #63, #66, #68, #72 are open run failures [R]. The architecture is exactly "own VIO + correction from the ground laptop", which is why it is worth knowing even though the code is not worth reviving.

## Map reuse has no published success rate, and the forums skew to failures

Family B looks attractive because the hangar is fixed and the ground laptop could build one map for all drones, but the evidence base is thin. NVIDIA's cuVSLAM documents `save_map` and `localize_in_map` with a position-only hint and says that on failure the node "will proceed to build a new map" ([concept page](https://nvidia-isaac-ros.github.io/concepts/visual_slam/cuvslam/index.html)), yet publishes **no relocalisation accuracy, radius, timing or success-rate figure**, and forum users report localisation failing even against the bag that built the map ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/cannot-localize-in-an-exist-map/328590)). SLAM mode is single-stereo-camera only per NVIDIA staff ([forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/using-isaac-ros-vslam-questions-on-localization-mode-pose-graph-saving-offline-and-3d-reconstruction-with-nvblox/295038)). Whether a map built with one D435i's intrinsics relocalises on another unit is UNVERIFIED anywhere. ORB-SLAM3's Atlas was built for in-process multi-session merging; the disk save/load was bolted on by the community and the 32-comment issue thread contains crashes and no confirmed relocalisation after load ([issue #128](https://github.com/UZ-SLAMLab/ORB_SLAM3/issues/128)); the only ROS 2 wrappers are third-party and Humble-centric ([Mechazo11](https://github.com/Mechazo11/ros2_orb_slam3)).

**cuVSLAM `localize_in_map`.** Pros: GPU-native, already the kind of VIO the Starlings may run, vendor-maintained. Cons: Jazzy on Orin requires Isaac ROS 4.6 on JetPack 7.2 (Aug 2026) and 5.0 already moved to ROS 2 Lyrical ([releases](https://nvidia-isaac-ros.github.io/releases/index.html)); orientation hint ignored; map format undocumented; no evidence of pure "localise-only in frozen map" mode (UNVERIFIED). Cost (ours): one day if the JetPack version already matches, otherwise a reflash first. Expectation: low; budget it as a test, not a plan.

**ORB-SLAM3 Atlas.** Pros: 3.6 cm stereo-inertial on EuRoC and camera-agnostic ORB/DBoW2 in principle [P]. Cons: GPLv3, V1.0 from December 2021 with 541 open issues, save/load crashes, no Jazzy build confirmed, Orin NX runtime unknown [R]. Cross-unit intrinsics reuse UNVERIFIED. Cost (ours): weeks of fork-hardening for an uncertain result; not recommended.

## Own VIO plus UWB alignment works, but yaw needs more than ranges

Family C is the recommended path and the one with the clearest reference design. Omni-swarm estimates **4-DoF relative poses** (roll and pitch come from gravity in each drone's VIO), fuses 100 Hz UWB ranges with odometry, inter-drone visual loop closures and CNN drone detection, and reaches 6–7 cm relative error indoors ([paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04131)). Its ablations are the most useful numbers in this review: **removing UWB barely changes relative error (0.062 → 0.063 m on Parallel1)**, while removing visual loop closures leaves relative error similar but doubles absolute error on some runs (0.086 → 0.225 m) [P Table VI]. The lab's plan, positions plus ranges with no shared visual features, is the Omni-swarm "UWB-odometry" sub-problem, for which the paper's closest proxies are the PGO and Xu2020 columns at **0.11–0.13 m at best and 0.19–0.96 m in parallel flight** [P]. There is no ROS 2 Jazzy package for this; DC-VIRO is MATLAB-only with no code ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19173)), DTVIRM-Swarm's details are UNVERIFIED, and mr-radio-localization is a UGV–UAV pair without VIO ([repo](https://github.com/amartinezsilva/mr-radio-localization)). The lab writes its own estimator, which is a smaller job than porting any Family A system.

**Omni-swarm (reference).** Reusable design numbers [P]: 4-DoF relative state; UWB outlier gates at 37° elevation and 0.3 m distance; initialisation needs a 1.5 × 1.5 × 0.8 m motion box and took 1.29–1.43 s with 100 % success when a visual match was available; back-end keeps at most 100 swarm keyframes; state+range channel 49 B at 100 Hz. Code is dead: ROS 1, TensorRT 7.1, RTX 3080-bound Docker, no replies since October 2022 [R]. Nooploop is the only supported UWB but the exact module and current LinkTrack protocol compatibility of `inf_uwb_ros` are UNVERIFIED ([driver](https://github.com/HKUST-Swarm/inf_uwb_ros)).

**DC-VIRO.** Right-invariant EKF on Lie groups that includes anchor positions in the state and shares anchor observations between robots; sharing cut RMSE from 0.205 to 0.147 m on one trajectory [P Table III]. Simulation only, 4 robots, 1 px camera noise, 70 % link probability [P]. Value to the lab: the consistency argument (keep the unobservable subspace state-independent) when designing a filter-based aligner.

**DTVIRM-Swarm.** Abstract-only: anchor-free, sliding-window EKF, chi-square gating of UWB ranges, MDS-MAP swarm-geometry initialisation, covariance intersection for cross-correlations, magnetometer in the loop ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/drones10010049)). The magnetometer is presumably how it resolves shared heading without anchors (inference; UNVERIFIED). Real-flight status, drone count and errors UNVERIFIED until someone downloads the PDF.

**MRS estimator switching.** Each localisation source is its own estimator plugin with its own TF origin; the controller's reference is re-expressed in the new frame at the switch, so "it is not noticeable to an outside observer" ([MBZIRC paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.05946)). The ROS 2 code pauses publishing in `ESTIMATOR_SWITCHING_STATE`, swaps the active pointer, and auto-switches to the first healthy estimator in a priority list or triggers failsafe landing ([estimation_manager.cpp](https://github.com/ctu-mrs/mrs_uav_managers/blob/ros2/src/estimation_manager/estimation_manager.cpp)). Map to the lab: `vio_origin`, `swarm_origin`, `map_origin` (and `mocap_origin` in the hangar) as parallel estimators. The ControlManager side of the "virtual jump" was not read; UNVERIFIED how the tracker is re-initialised.

**Fishberg & How.** Four LinkTrack P tags per robot at 0.35 m radius give 2-D relative pose including heading from ranges alone, 3.95–5.88° mean heading error after calibration, with heading spikes to 104° during fast counter-yawing [P]. This is the hardware answer to yaw observability, but each extra tag consumes LinkTrack node capacity (see the UWB table).

### Is yaw observable from UWB ranges alone?

The evidence says no in the cases the lab cares about. Omni-swarm states that UWB-odometry relative localisation "can become unobservable in some cases, e.g., parallel flight tasks", that "one moving drone and one fixed drone gives position but not yaw of the fixed drone", and that its initialiser needs at least 3-DoF observability and a 1.5 × 1.5 × 0.8 m motion box; its UWB+odometry predecessor achieved 0 % initialisation success in 52–84 s on the Parallel1 run ([paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04131)). DC-VIRO's Lemma 1 confirms that global position and yaw remain unobservable for VIO plus ranging to estimated anchors, which is the normal gauge freedom; the paper does not separately prove what the anchors add to inter-robot yaw ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19173)). Practical systems resolve inter-drone yaw in one of four ways: a common heading sensor (DTVIRM's magnetometer, fragile indoors; UNVERIFIED detail), bearing or visual detection of the other drone (Omni-swarm, CREPES-X at 7.0 cm / 2.2° RMSE ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24688)), ZJU active mutual observation ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01292))), multiple tags per vehicle so range differences encode heading (Fishberg & How, 4–6° [P]), or relative motion excitation over time. With one LinkTrack tag per drone and formation flight, where the drones move in parallel and keep their relative geometry, the yaw between VIO frames will not converge from ranges; the estimator must either be initialised with an excitation manoeuvre, given a common start pad, fed a bearing cue, or use fixed LinkTrack anchors in the hangar (an inference, not a published result: anchors make each drone's position observable in a shared frame, after which the yaw of each VIO frame becomes observable from that drone's own motion). Note also that no paper publishes a yaw error for the UWB-odometry-only configuration; a user asked Omni-swarm for exactly this and got no answer ([issue #12](https://github.com/HKUST-Aerial-Robotics/Omni-swarm/issues/12)).

Two cautions from the notes carry into the simulator design. First, no one has measured LinkTrack ranging noise in flight on a quadrotor (propeller, airframe and altitude effects); Fishberg's biases are ground-robot measurements. Second, the in-band LinkTrack data channel (8–160 kbps) can carry a 49-byte state-plus-range packet at 100 Hz but not keyframes, so WiFi remains the carrier for anything richer, exactly as in Omni-swarm.

## What to test before the early-November review

The decision gate is the lab's own VIO drift. Fly one Starling the typical mission profile in the hangar with OptiTrack running and compute position and yaw drift against mocap over time and distance; this number decides whether Family C alone is enough (Omni-swarm's ablations suggest decimetre relative error without visual cues) or whether a Swarm-SLAM spike is justified. Kimera-Multi's table shows that loop closures earn their cost at 15–49 m of accumulated drift, so if the hangar mission drifts by centimetres, Family A is solving a problem the lab does not have ([T-RO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386)).

In simulation, build the drift-and-range generator from the table above: per-drone translation random walk at 0.3–1 % of distance, yaw random walk sized to give roughly 1–2° RMS over a two-minute flight (labelled as a proxy, since no per-minute figure exists), UWB ranges with a 5 cm Gaussian core plus a per-pair constant bias drawn from 2–28 cm, wall and body-occlusion spikes of +0.3 m or more, and a rate that follows the LinkTrack capacity table. The UWBPX4Sim Gazebo plugin and the mr-radio-localization PGO node are the nearest ROS 2 Jazzy starting points ([UWBPX4Sim](https://github.com/amartinezsilva/UWBPX4Sim)). Test the 4-DoF aligner on three motion classes: an excitation manoeuvre at start (Omni-swarm's 1.5 × 1.5 × 0.8 m box), the lab's actual formation choreography (parallel motion, the degenerate case), and a hover-plus-one-mover case; record whether yaw converges, how long it takes, and what the position error does when it does not. Repeat with one versus four tags per drone, and with versus without a fixed anchor pair, to see what buys yaw observability at the least node-capacity cost. Finally, simulate an estimator switch (VIO frame → swarm frame → mocap frame) with the MRS pattern and confirm the commanded setpoint does not move.

On recorded data, run the aligner on MILUV's `default_3_*` three-drone runs with each drone's own VIO (any of OpenVINS, VINS-Fusion or cuVSLAM) and score inter-drone relative error with evo against Vicon; the bar is MILUV's own VIO+UWB EKF at 10–20 cm ([MILUV](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14376)). Pull the Omni-swarm Dropbox folder, run `rosbag info`, and check what the Nooploop messages look like against the current LinkTrack driver output. If the Swarm-SLAM spike is approved, use EuRoC MH01–05 as five pseudo-agents, the convention COVINS established, to get a C-SLAM baseline that needs no new hardware. Try cuVSLAM `localize_in_map` for one day only if the Starlings are already on JetPack 7.2 with Isaac ROS 4.6; save a map with one drone, call `localize_in_map` from a second drone standing at a known OptiTrack pose, and record success rate and error, expecting failures given the forum record.

## Conclusion

The literature pushes the lab towards a design, not a download: no system matches the stack, and the one that comes closest to the lab's idea (Omni-swarm) got its accuracy from visual inter-drone matching rather than from UWB, which means the real design question is how the swarm frame gets its yaw. The honest reading of the evidence is that UWB ranges with one tag per drone will hold inter-drone position to roughly a decimetre once aligned, but the alignment itself needs an excitation, an anchor, a bearing cue or more tags, and formation flight is exactly the motion that gives none of these for free. That shifts the November conversation from "which repository" to "which yaw cue costs least", with the LinkTrack capacity table making the trade explicit: more tags means lower rate.

The other shift is in what collaborative SLAM is for. Every quantified gain in the Family A papers belongs to kilometre-scale, drifting, revisiting trajectories; the hangar is the opposite environment, with OptiTrack available to measure exactly how much drift the lab actually has. If that measurement comes back at centimetres per mission, the alignment layer is a maintenance item rather than a research problem, and the saved effort is better spent on the UWB calibration routine and the estimator-switching logic that every option needs anyway.
