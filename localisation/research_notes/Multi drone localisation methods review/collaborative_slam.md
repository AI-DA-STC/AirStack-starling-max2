# Open-source collaborative SLAM systems: Swarm-SLAM, Kimera-Multi, COVINS / COVINS-G — facts and validation evidence (as of 2026-10-22)

Scope note. Target platform for the verdicts: swarm of ModalAI Starling 2 Max drones, each with Jetson Orin NX 16 GB, Intel RealSense D435i (stereo IR + RGB-D + IMU), ROS 2 Jazzy, WiFi between drones, ground laptop with RTX GPU. Comparison framing: "collaborative SLAM with map sharing" vs "own VIO per drone + lightweight frame alignment".

Method note. GitHub numbers were pulled from the public GitHub REST API on 2026-10-22 (unauthenticated, from this machine). Paper numbers were read from the arXiv PDFs via pdftotext. ## Swarm-SLAM (MISTLab, Polytechnique Montréal; Lajoie & Beltrame, RA-L vol. 9 no. 1, 2024; arXiv 2301.06230)

### Takeaway
Swarm-SLAM is the only one of the three that is (a) ROS 2 native, (b) demonstrated live on robots with NVIDIA Jetson onboard computers plus RealSense D455 cameras, and (c) still receiving maintainer attention in 2025–2026. It is a sparse, decentralized back-end (pose-graph + GNC) that takes odometry from an external front-end (RTAB-Map in the paper); it does not share dense maps. Its weaknesses for the lab: the real-robot demo had no ground truth (so no real-world ATE), it has never been shown on drones, a third-party benchmark found it degrades in dynamic scenes, and the paper reports no single-robot-VIO-vs-collaborative ATE comparison.

**What is shared; bandwidth; centralized vs distributed**
- Architecture: decentralized, two-stage inter-robot loop closure: compact global descriptors are exchanged first (ScanContext for lidar, CosPlace for images), then only for prioritized candidates are local features/keypoints transmitted for geometric verification; pose-graph optimization is run by a "decentrally-elected robot" ("a robot is dynamically elected among the robots in communication range … The other robots share their current pose graph estimates with the elected robot and receive the updated estimates"). Back-end = Graduated Non-Convexity with Truncated Least Squares. — [arXiv 2301.06230v3 PDF, Secs. IV–V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06230v3)
- Table II (communication in kB for whole dataset run, GNC = Swarm-SLAM's back-end vs DGS+PCM vs D-GNC baselines using the same front-end): KITTI 00 (2 robots) 280.00 vs 30045.50 vs 13499.49; KITTI-360 09 (5) 484.91 vs 3241.21 vs 4485.13; GrAco Ground (3) 105.82 vs 44686.69 vs 78162.42; M2DGR Gate (3) 51.29 vs 242.02 vs 721.39; S3E Square (3) 80.97 vs 1261.12 vs 620.19; S3E College (3) 150.37 vs 3420.23 vs 3012.28. — [arXiv 2301.06230v3 PDF, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06230v3)
- Real-world: 94.95 MB total between 3 robots for 475 m / 3103 keyframes, "excluding the visualization. The communication load is mostly attributable to the front-end and thus dependent on the number of keyframes." — [arXiv 2301.06230v3 PDF, Sec. VI-B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.06230v3)
- Deployment transport recommended by the authors: Zenoh DDS bridge (`zenoh-bridge-dds` / `zenoh-plugin-ros2dds`) with per-robot `ROS_DOMAIN_ID`, bridging only `/cslam/.*` topics. — [cslam_experiments README](https://github.com/lajoiepy/cslam_experiments); [startup instructions](https://lajoiepy.github.io/cslam_documentation/html/md_startup_instructions.html)

### Inferences
- The Starling stack maps naturally onto Swarm-SLAM's design: run any ROS 2 VIO (RTAB-Map, OpenVINS, cuVSLAM) per drone, feed odometry + RGB-D keyframes to `cslam`, and let the elected drone (or the ground laptop acting as a peer) run GNC. The CosPlace descriptor needs Torch; Orin NX has CUDA, so the image front-end should run but has not been documented on Orin — budget time for Torch/CUDA wheel matching on JetPack.
- Communication figures (tens to hundreds of kB per dataset run; ~95 MB for 475 m with 3 robots incl. front-end) are compatible with WiFi between drones, which is the regime the system was built for (ad-hoc OpenWrt gateways in the paper).
- The lack of GPS/ground-truth in the real-world demo and the absence of a "single-robot VIO vs collaborative" ATE table mean the paper does not quantify how much the inter-robot loop closures help in absolute terms; the lab would have to measure this itself (mocap arena is suitable).
- Third-party results (CSE dynamic scenes with ATE up to 13.7 m; GRAND-SLAM's 2.5–4.7 cm on Replica vs <1 cm for dense competitors) suggest accuracy is adequate but not best-in-class, and that it relies heavily on front-end quality.

### Gaps
- No paper or issue found where Swarm-SLAM runs on a drone/UAV; no Jetson Orin build report found.
- No reported real-world ATE (no ground truth in the parking-lot test).
- No per-agent kB/s bandwidth figure (only totals per run).
- Full text of the 2026 planetary-analogue paper and the 2602.02430 paper was not read; their quantitative findings are not captured.
- S3E paper's Swarm-SLAM/COVINS numbers were not retrieved.

## Kimera-Multi (MIT SPARK Lab + ACL; Tian, Chang, Herrera Arias, Nieto-Granda, How, Carlone; T-RO vol. 38 no. 4, 2022; arXiv 2106.14386; field paper IROS 2023, arXiv 2304.04362)

### Takeaway
Kimera-Multi has the strongest real-robot validation of the three (up to 8 Clearpath Jackals, ~7.8 km combined, RealSense D455/D435i, Intel NUC, real 2.4 GHz WiFi with up to 45 % packet drop) and the clearest quantified benefit over single-robot VIO (end-to-end error 14.8–24.6 m for Kimera-VIO vs 0.01–0.13 m after Kimera-Multi on Medfield). But it is ROS 1 (Noetic/Ubuntu 20.04), has no licence file on the index repo, requires its own Kimera-VIO front-end, has not been ported to ROS 2 or shown on Jetson/ARM (a Kimera-VIO-ROS issue reports it "very slow" on Jetson Nano), its multi-master "Remote Topic Manager" is still listed as "Coming Soon", and the distributed back-end takes minutes to converge on 8-robot problems.

### Cited Findings

**Real robots or simulation; robots, hardware, length, environment; drones?**
- T-RO paper: three photo-realistic DCIST simulations (Medfield 2396 m, City 1213 m, Camp 1037 m total trajectory, 3 robots each), EuRoC Vicon Room 1/2 (3 robots, 211/206 m) and Machine Hall (5 robots, 466 m), and two real outdoor datasets collected with "a Clearpath Jackal UGV equipped with a forward-facing RealSense D435i RGBD Camera and IMU": Medfield State Hospital (3 trajectories, longest 860 m) and MIT Stata Center (3 trajectories, each >500 m). — [arXiv 2106.14386 PDF, Sec. VII-B/C, Tables I & V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386)
- Field paper (IROS 2023): "a fleet of Clearpath Jackal rovers equipped with a Realsense D455 RGB-D Camera and an Ouster or Velodyne 3D lidar … Each robot has an Intel NUC computer with an Intel i7 4.70 GHz processor, and communicates with the other robots via a 2.4 GHz wireless network. Each robot was tele-operated." Datasets: Campus-Outdoor (6 robots, 6044 m, ~20 min), Campus-Tunnels (8 robots, 6753 m, ~30 min), Campus-Hybrid (8 robots, 7785 m, ~27 min, multi-level). Ground truth: lidar reference map + differential GPS, trajectories via LOCUS 2.0. Live field test: 8 robots, 3 suffered hardware failures (2 battery, 1 camera offline), system still estimated the remaining 5. — [arXiv 2304.04362 PDF, Secs. IV–VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04362); [Kimera-Multi-Data README](https://github.com/MIT-SPARK/Kimera-Multi-Data)
- No UAV/drone experiments in either paper (all ground robots or EuRoC/DCIST data). — [arXiv 2106.14386 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386); [arXiv 2304.04362 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04362)

**What is shared; bandwidth; centralized vs distributed**
- Fully distributed, peer-to-peer. Exchanged: DBoW2 bag-of-words vectors of keyframes (place recognition), then ORB keypoints + descriptors for candidate matches (geometric verification), then "public poses" during distributed PGO (D-GNC built on RBCD). Field version: every n_b-th BoW vector transmitted (default n_b = 3), pose graph coarsened to d = 2 m ("on average 90 % smaller"), communication via "a remote topic manager … on top of the UDP protocol" using the ENet library. — [arXiv 2106.14386 PDF, Secs. IV–V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386); [arXiv 2304.04362 PDF, Sec. III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04362)
- T-RO Table II, communication [MB] per whole run (PR + GV + DPGO = Total vs centralized-keypoints vs centralized-images): Medfield 22.6 + 41.5 + 1.8 = 65.9 vs 141 vs 2113; City 69.5 vs 155 vs 2326; Camp 59.4 vs 246 vs 3685; Vicon Room 1 27.8 vs 81.7 vs 1226; Vicon Room 2 24.4 vs 83.9 vs 1259; Machine Hall (5 robots) 145.7 vs 157 vs 2362. "on the Vicon Room 2 dataset, our system achieves a communication reduction of 70 % compared to the baseline centralized system that transmits all keypoints and descriptors." — [arXiv 2106.14386 PDF, Table II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386)
- Field paper Table II (real 2.4 GHz WiFi, 6–8 robots): bandwidth Max/Avg MB/s: Tunnels 2.10/0.55, Hybrid 0.41/0.12, Outdoor 0.10/0.01; packet drop 45.55 %, 21.50 %, 20.28 %; max delay 7.71 s / 4.17 s / 1.53 s. Lesson: "in scenarios such as the Campus-Tunnels dataset where there are many loop closures, the front-end sometimes uses most of the available bandwidth for exchanging visual keypoints and descriptors, causing network congestion that interferes with the distributed back-end." — [arXiv 2304.04362 PDF, Table II & Sec. VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04362)

**ATE / inter-robot error; improvement vs single-robot VIO**
- T-RO Table I, ATE [m] vs ground truth (L2 / PCM / D-GNC / Centralized GNC): Medfield 64.2 / 12.5 / 3.92 / 3.88; City 3.58 / 1.57 / 0.85 / 1.00; Camp 11.9 / 1.37 / 0.96 / 1.33; Vicon Room 1 1.17 / 1.00 / 0.35 / 0.36; Vicon Room 2 1.87 / 1.56 / 0.47 / 0.43; Machine Hall 1.92 / 1.76 / 0.41 / 0.52. — [arXiv 2106.14386 PDF, Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386)
- T-RO Table V, end-to-end position error [m] on outdoor datasets (Kimera-VIO alone / Kimera-Multi / Centralized): Medfield robot 0 (600 m) 18.74 / 0.01 / 0.01; robot 1 (860 m) 14.84 / 0.13 / 0.13; robot 2 (728 m) 24.55 / 0.09 / 0.09. Stata robot 0 (515 m) 49.02 / 0.03 / 0.01; robot 1 (570 m) 24.19 / 33.13 / 21.56; robot 2 (610 m) 29.35 / 1.26 / 1.17. "Kimera-VIO accumulates a drift of approximately 15–25 m on each trajectory sequence … mostly in the vertical direction." On Stata, default D-GNC left one robot misaligned (only inter-robot loop closure rejected); fixing it needed 2000 RBCD iterations = 14 minutes vs 120 iterations = 50 s default. Medfield global graph 15 650 poses, 100 RBCD iterations = 53 s. — [arXiv 2106.14386 PDF, Table V & Sec. VII-C](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.14386)
- Field paper Table I (Full communication, 3 trials): Distributed ATE (m) Tunnels 4.48 ± 0.29, Hybrid 7.71 ± 0.78, Outdoor 12.4 ± 1.92; Centralized 4.38 ± 0.21, 5.83 ± 0.16, 9.38 ± 0.31. Average map error (AME) Dist 2.62/1.9/4.83 m vs Cent 2.58/1.58/4.99 m. Distributed optimization time avg 139.52 s / 174.05 s / 100.92 s vs centralized 3.24 s / 1.65 s / 0.55 s. Inlier ratio of detected loop closures only 20.8 %, 26.9 %, 36.5 %. — [arXiv 2304.04362 PDF, Table I](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04362)
- Field paper lessons: BoW place recognition "often detects too many matches indoor and not enough outdoor"; "Kimera-Multi only performs visual loop closure detection, which is sensitive to changes in the viewpoint … some robots visit the same locations in opposite directions … no loop closure is detected and no trajectory correction occurs"; "the accuracy of distributed GNC comes at the cost of increased runtime"; loose termination (rel = 1 m) produced a visible failure in a live Campus-Tunnels test. — [arXiv 2304.04362 PDF, Sec. VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04362)

## COVINS (ETH Zurich V4RL; Schmuck, Ziegler, Karrer, Perraudin, Chli; ISMAR-Adjunct 2021; arXiv 2108.05756) and COVINS-G (Patel, Karrer, Bänninger, Chli; ICRA 2023; arXiv 2301.07147)

### Takeaway
COVINS/COVINS-G is a centralized server back-end (agents run VIO onboard, server does place recognition, PGO, optional GBA). It has the best EuRoC multi-agent accuracy numbers (2.4–4.2 cm ATE with ORB-SLAM3 front-end) and COVINS-G accepts any VIO (tested OpenVINS, VINS-Mono, ORB-SLAM3, SVO-Pro, RealSense T265 — heterogeneous mixes in one mission). But all evaluation is EuRoC replay on Intel NUCs, a 12-"UAV" run in a photo-realistic simulator, and hand-held D455 walks — no real drone flights. The repository (moved to v4rl-ucy) is ROS 1 Melodic/Noetic, GPLv3, last committed 2023-05-28, with 18 open issues dominated by run failures, and a third-party benchmark reported "numerous failures". It is effectively unmaintained.

### Cited Findings

**Real robots or simulation; robots, hardware, length, environment; drones?**
- COVINS experiments: EuRoC MH01–05 (5 agents) and V101–103 (3 agents), "re-playing data in real-time" with agents on "Intel NUC 7i7BNH with 3.5 GHz × 4 … connected to the server via a wireless network, so that real communication takes place"; Local server: Lenovo T480s; Cloud server: AWS c5a.8xlarge (32 vCPUs). — [arXiv 2108.05756 PDF, Sec. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05756)
- 12-agent experiment: "a newly generated dataset with 12 UAVs equipped with a downward looking camera flying over a small village … created using the visual-inertial simulator from [21], creating photo-realistic vision datasets for UAV flights … 12 circular UAV trajectories of 20 m radius, covering an area of about 500 m² with 1750 m total trajectory length … over 3200 KFs and about 200k LMs. The average ATE of the estimate is 0.050 m, the average scale error is 0.44 %." (Simulated drones, not real.) — [arXiv 2108.05756 PDF, Sec. 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05756)
- COVINS-G experiments: EuRoC MH/V1 with 3–5 agents (Sec. IV-B); real-world outdoor: "Hand-held Intel RealSense D455 … Four sequences … walking sidewalks near ETH Zurich, covering about 67 500 m²" (Sec. IV-C); 2-agent run with D455 (VINS-Mono) + T265 (Sec. IV-D). Agents: Intel NUC 7i7BNH @ 3.5 GHz; server: laptop i7-6700HQ @ 2.6 GHz. No UAV flights. — [arXiv 2301.07147v3 HTML, Sec. IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.07147v3)

**What is shared; bandwidth; centralized vs distributed**
- Centralized: "each agent runs visual-inertial odometry onboard … shares map data with a central server back-end, which runs on a local PC or remote cloud". Agents send keyframes (2D keypoints + descriptors, IMU pre-integration) and landmarks (map points) as `MsgKeyframe`/`MsgLandmark`; "full" vs "update" messages; batched at 5 Hz; server→agent 2 Hz drift-corrected pose of latest KF. Message sizes: "KF full: 97 kB; KF update: 273 byte; LM full: 162 byte; LM update: 65 byte". — [arXiv 2108.05756 PDF, Secs. 4.5 & 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05756); [covins README](https://github.com/VIS4ROB-lab/covins)
- COVINS bandwidth (Table 3): agent→server avg 493.36 kB/s over 8 EuRoC sequences (MH1 422.83, MH5 540.37, V103 609.22 kB/s); server→agent 2.31 kB/s; "approximately between 400 and 600 KB/s, which can comfortably be covered by typical WiFi infrastructure"; KF creation 2.9 KFs/s (MH1) to 6.9 KFs/s (V103); communication time ~1 s per sequence (<1 % overhead). — [arXiv 2108.05756 PDF, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.05756)
- COVINS-G: agents send only keyframes with 2D keypoints + descriptors (no map points), ~179.74 kB/s per agent vs 486.41 kB/s for COVINS (Table III); loop-closure computation 213 ± 171 ms (75 loops) vs COVINS 35 ± 23 ms (57 loops), "real-time operation for up to 5 loop computations per second". No GBA possible in COVINS-G because the server has no map points. — [arXiv 2301.07147v3 HTML, Table III](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.07147v3); [covins README](https://github.com/VIS4ROB-lab/covins)

### Inferences
- Architecturally COVINS-G is the closest match to "own VIO per drone + alignment on the ground laptop": each drone keeps its own VIO, the laptop server fuses keyframes and sends back a drift correction at 2 Hz. But because it is ROS 1, GPLv3, unmaintained since mid-2023 and failure-prone per issues and the CSE benchmark, reimplementing its idea (server-side place recognition + PGO over 2D keypoints/descriptors) in ROS 2 would likely cost less than reviving the codebase.
- Per-agent bandwidth of ~180 kB/s (COVINS-G) to ~500 kB/s (COVINS) at 3–7 keyframes/s is fine for one WiFi link but will contend with ModalAI telemetry/video if several drones stream to one laptop simultaneously.
- The EuRoC "multi-agent" evaluation is the standard trick (one EuRoC sequence per agent) and is exactly what the lab could reproduce offline for any candidate, including a home-grown alignment scheme.

## Cross-system comparison and verdicts for the Starling 2 Max swarm

### Takeaway
None of the three has been validated on drones with Jetson + RealSense; only Swarm-SLAM is ROS 2 and still alive. The documented benefit of collaborative SLAM over per-robot VIO is large only for long, drifting trajectories (hundreds of metres, Kimera-Multi outdoor: 15–49 m VIO drift → ≤1.3 m) and small or unmeasured for short indoor runs; against a mocap-equipped arena the "own VIO + lightweight frame alignment" option is cheaper and the best-supported offline test (EuRoC-as-agents, Kimera-Multi-Data D455 bags) is the same for both options.

### Cited Findings
- Platform constraint: JetPack 6.x on Orin is Ubuntu 22.04-based and ROS 2 Jazzy has Tier-1 support only on Ubuntu 24.04, so Humble is the native fit on Orin NX (Jazzy needs a container). Swarm-SLAM's install script is "tested with Humble and Jazzy". — [NVIDIA forum thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/orin-nx-ros2/334966); [build-everything.sh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MISTLab/Swarm-SLAM/main/build-everything.sh)
- Summary table of repo health (GitHub API, 2026-10-22): Swarm-SLAM 715★ / 82 forks / 2 open / 54 closed / last push 2025-04-10 / MIT / ROS 2; Kimera-Multi 417★ / 53 / 11 open / 25 closed / 2025-01-28 / no licence file / ROS 1 Noetic; COVINS 465★ / 73 / 18 open / 47 closed / last commit 2023-05-28 / GPLv3 / ROS 1 Melodic–Noetic. — [Swarm-SLAM API](https://api.github.com/repos/MISTLab/Swarm-SLAM); [Kimera-Multi API](https://api.github.com/repos/MIT-SPARK/Kimera-Multi); [covins API](https://api.github.com/repos/v4rl-ucy/covins)
- Only third-party head-to-head found: CSE (Isaac Sim, 3 robots) ran Swarm-SLAM and COVINS; Swarm-SLAM stereo static ATE 0.07–0.41 m vs COVINS 2.0–9.4 m with multiple failures. Kimera-Multi was not included. — [arXiv 2411.14775 Table IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14775v1)
- Adjacent system worth knowing (not in scope, not researched in depth): D²SLAM (Xu, Liu, Chen, Shen; T-RO 2024, DOI 10.1109/TRO.2024.3422003, 79 OpenAlex citations) is a "decentralized and distributed collaborative visual-inertial SLAM system for aerial swarms" with near-field relative localization and stereo/omnidirectional front-end; drone count, compute and ROS version UNVERIFIED here. — [arXiv 2211.01538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01538); [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=Swarm-SLAM%20Sparse%20Decentralized%20Collaborative)

### Inferences (verdicts)
- **Swarm-SLAM — PARTLY fits.** Pros: ROS 2 (Humble/Jazzy tested), MIT, Jetson AGX Xavier + RealSense D455 already used by the authors, decentralized with low bandwidth, odometry front-end is pluggable (RTAB-Map in paper; any ROS 2 odometry topic). Cons: never shown on drones; no real-world ATE; CosPlace needs Torch on Orin; third-party results show weakness in dynamic scenes; open issue #59 (Apr 2026) about runtime problems after successful compile. Recommended as the only candidate worth a 1–2 week feasibility spike (Docker on Orin NX, D435i RGB-D front-end, 2 drones, mocap ground truth).
- **Kimera-Multi — NO for reuse as-is; YES as evidence and dataset source.** ROS 1 Noetic, mandatory Kimera-VIO, unreleased multi-master transport, no licence file, slow distributed back-end, no ROS 2/Jetson path. Its D455 8-robot datasets and its T-RO Table V are the best quantitative argument for when collaborative loop closures matter (>500 m drift-prone trajectories), which is the opposite regime from short indoor swarm flights.
- **COVINS / COVINS-G — NO.** ROS 1, GPLv3, last commit May 2023, many unresolved run-failure issues, third-party "numerous failures", no real drone or Jetson validation. Its architecture (per-agent VIO + ground-station server doing place recognition and PGO on 2D keypoints) is nonetheless the pattern to copy if the lab builds its own "VIO + frame alignment" layer; the EuRoC-as-agents protocol and the ~180 kB/s per-agent budget are useful design targets.
- **Decision support:** For a mocap-equipped lab with short indoor flights, the measured value-add of inter-robot loop closures is undocumented at that scale by all three papers; the lab should first measure per-drone VIO drift over its typical mission length and only pursue Swarm-SLAM if drift exceeds the alignment tolerance of the formation controller.

### Gaps
- No source found that evaluates any of the three on real multi-drone flights, on Jetson Orin, or under ROS 2 Jazzy; all such statements above are inferences.
- Google Scholar "cited by" lists could not be accessed; OpenAlex counts substitute.
- ROS Discourse and NVIDIA forums returned nothing specific to these three systems.
