# VIO + UWB swarm alignment, MRS estimator switching, and "localise in a saved map" (cuVSLAM / ORB-SLAM3)

Scope: factual notes to decide between (A) own VIO + continuous UWB-aided alignment, (B) own VIO + saved shared map, (C) collaborative SLAM, for a swarm of ModalAI Starling 2 Max (Jetson Orin NX, RealSense D435i, Nooploop LinkTrack UWB, ROS 2 Jazzy, WiFi). Facts as of 2026-10-22. Anything not confirmed from a primary source is marked UNVERIFIED. Numbers are quoted from the cited source; where a fetch could only see part of a paper, this is stated.

## KQ1. 2024–2026 work fusing per-drone VIO with inter-drone UWB ranges (DC-VIRO, DTVIRM-Swarm, others) and open-source packages

### Takeaway
The two named 2026 papers are both filter-based, anchor-aware or anchor-free VIO+UWB fusions, but neither releases code and DC-VIRO is simulation-only (4 robots, MATLAB). The only *proven on real drones, open-source* VIO+UWB swarm estimator remains HKUST's Omni-swarm (ROS 1, Jetson TX2, Nooploop UWB, 2–4 drones, ~6 cm relative position / 1.1–4.3 deg relative rotation error). No ROS 2 Jazzy package that fuses per-drone VIO with inter-drone UWB for a swarm frame was found; the closest ROS 2 Jazzy code (mr-radio-localization / UWBPX4Sim, ICRA 2026) is a UGV–UAV pair using radar/wheel odometry rather than VIO.

### Cited Findings

**DC-VIRO (arXiv 2602.19173, Feb 2026)**
- Title "Distributed and Consistent Multi-Robot Visual-Inertial-Ranging Odometry on Lie Groups", authors Ziwei Kang (North China Electric Power University, Beijing) and Yizhi Zhou (George Mason University, corresponding); submitted 22 Feb 2026; CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19173); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19173)
- Method: tightly fuses VIO with UWB ranging across robots; UWB *anchor positions are included in the estimated state* to handle calibration uncertainty; robots exchange *shared anchor observations* as extra geometric constraints; right-invariant error formulation on Lie groups to keep the unobservable subspace state-independent (consistency). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19173)
- Experiments: **simulation only, MATLAB, 3-D**; four robots (Table II lists Robots 1–4); three UWB anchors in Table I (text says "33 UWB anchors" — internal inconsistency, likely a typo for 3); IMU 100 Hz, monocular camera 10 Hz, UWB tag 10 Hz; camera noise 1 px; initial anchor position noise 0.1 m; 100 Monte-Carlo runs; pairwise inter-robot communication with 70% probability of link. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19173)
- What is shared: UWB ranges to common anchors and visual feature observations for common features. No bandwidth figures. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19173)
- Results (Table III, average RMSE position m / orientation deg): with inter-robot info — trajectory a 0.147 m / 1.295 deg, trajectory b 0.256 m / 1.027 deg; without inter-robot info — 0.205 m / 1.788 deg and 0.498 m / 1.575 deg. Only baseline is the same estimator without sharing. No NEES, no anchor-calibration error numbers, no per-robot numbers in text. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19173)
- Observability: Lemma 1 — for general 3-D motion the observability matrix has **four unobservable directions (global position and yaw)**; the right-invariant form keeps this subspace independent of the estimate. The paper does not separately prove what the anchors add. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19173)
- Code: **none released** (no repository URL in abstract page or paper text). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19173)

**DTVIRM-Swarm (Drones 10(1):49, Jan 2026)**
- Full title "DTVIRM-Swarm: A Distributed and Tightly Integrated Visual-Inertial-UWB-Magnetic System for Anchor Free Swarm Cooperative Localization"; authors Xin-Can Luo, Xueyu Du, Shuai Yue, Yun-Xiao Lv, Lilian Zhang, Xiao-Feng He, Wenqi Wu, Jun Mao; published 9 Jan 2026; DOI 10.3390/drones10010049; 4 citations at the time of the Semantic Scholar lookup. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/drones10010049); [Semantic Scholar API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/drones10010049?fields=title,authors,year,abstract,publicationDate,externalIds,citationCount)
- Method (abstract): GNSS-free, **anchor-free** UAV-swarm localisation; per-UAV IMU + **magnetometer** + monocular camera + inter-UAV UWB ranging fused in a **sliding-window EKF**; chi-squared outlier rejection on UWB ranges; "pose-only" visual measurement model; **MDS-MAP initialisation** of the swarm geometry using ranging + inertial + geomagnetic data; **Covariance Intersection** to handle unknown cross-correlations between UAV estimates; claims continued operation when vision fails. — [Semantic Scholar API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/drones10010049?fields=title,authors,year,abstract,publicationDate,externalIds,citationCount)
- Yaw observability (inference from the abstract, see Inferences): the magnetometer supplies a common heading reference, which is how an anchor-free, range-only inter-drone constraint can be made to resolve the shared yaw; this is why the system is "VIRM" (visual-inertial-ranging-magnetic). UNVERIFIED in detail — full text could not be fetched (MDPI returned HTTP 403 to all fetch attempts, including a browser user-agent).
- Real vs simulation, number of drones, numeric relative-position / yaw errors, code release: **UNVERIFIED / not obtainable** from the abstract. The abstract only says it "outperforms state-of-the-art approaches in accuracy, efficiency, and robustness". — [Semantic Scholar API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/drones10010049?fields=title,authors,year,abstract,publicationDate,externalIds,citationCount)

**UAV–UGV anchor-free VIO–UWB fusion (Drones 10(3):175, Mar 2026)**
- "UAV–UGV Collaborative Localization in GNSS-Denied Large-Scale Environments: An Anchor-Free VIO–UWB Fusion with Adaptive Weighting and Outlier Suppression", Hao-Yuan Xu, Gao-Peng Zhao, Yu-Ming Bo, published 4 Mar 2026. Factor-graph back-end; stage 1 uses covariance-based drift bounds + Mahalanobis gate to reject bad UWB ranges early; stage 2 adaptively estimates UWB confidence from residuals during global optimisation. **Field test: 3 UAVs + 2 UGVs over ~1.6 km²**; localisation RMSE reduced by ~24.6% and max error by ~31.2% versus an outlier-rejection-only variant. No absolute error values or code in the abstract. — [MDPI page](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446X/10/3/175); [Semantic Scholar API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3390/drones10030175?fields=title,authors,year,abstract,publicationDate,externalIds)

**Omni-swarm (HKUST, T-RO 2022; still the reference real-drone VIO+UWB swarm system)**
- Decentralised; fisheye stereo VIO (VINS-Fisheye, FoV up to 235 deg) + UWB ranging + visual drone detection + map-based (loop-closure) relative localisation, fused by graph optimisation; relative states estimated as **4-DoF poses**; "all the communications between the devices are through UWB broadcast". — [ar5iv 2103.04131](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04131)
- Hardware: DJI Manifold 2-G (Nvidia TX2); "NoopLoop UWB module"; 2 drones in the indoor/outdoor datasets, up to 4 in plug-and-play and collision-avoidance tests. — [ar5iv 2103.04131](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04131)
- Bandwidth per drone (Table III): total 59.4 kB/s = Odom & Dis 4.90 kB/s + Keyframe 54.0 kB/s + Inliers 0.47 kB/s. — [ar5iv 2103.04131](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04131)
- Relative error (RE), real flights: position 0.062 m (Parallel1), 0.063 m (Parallel2), 0.072 m (Parallel3), 0.069 m (RandFlight), 0.057 m (Outdoor Parallel1), 0.111 m (Outdoor RandFlight); rotation RE 1.1–4.3 deg across datasets. VIO-only Parallel1 position RE 0.261 m; the 2020 predecessor (Xu2020) 0.959 m. Outdoor ~235 m run: 1.9 m drift vs 6.35 m for VIO alone. Works "even in totally non-line-of-sight cases" once a drone revisits the other's start location (wall-separated test with UWB disabled). — [ar5iv 2103.04131](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.04131)
- Code: github.com/HKUST-Aerial-Robotics/Omni-swarm, **GPLv3**, **ROS 1** tooling (roslaunch; distribution not named), officially supported on Jetson TX2 / Ubuntu 18.04, "NoopLoop UWB module only"; needs swarm_msgs, inf_uwb_ros, VINS-Fisheye, TensorRT CNN models; 291 stars / 46 forks / 13 open issues; 1,779 commits on branch `pinhole_compatiable`; README promises an ONNX frontend "very soon" with no date. — [GitHub Omni-swarm](https://github.com/HKUST-Aerial-Robotics/Omni-swarm)
- Successor D²SLAM (T-RO vol. 40, 2024, DOI 10.1109/TRO.2024.3422003): decentralised collaborative visual-inertial SLAM with near-field/far-field modes; **LGPL-3**; **ROS 1** tooling; Docker images for PC and Jetson (evaluated on Xavier NX); needs CUDA/TensorRT, SuperPoint + NetVLAD models; quad-fisheye rig default, RealSense D435 launch provided; 474 stars / 70 forks / 613 commits; **README does not describe UWB use** (only an excluded `/uwb_node/incoming_broadcast_data` topic). — [GitHub D2SLAM](https://github.com/HKUST-Aerial-Robotics/D2SLAM)

**Other 2024–2026 relative-localisation work (for context; not all use UWB+VIO)**
- CREPES-X (arXiv 2512.24688, v1 31 Dec 2025, v2 14 Sep 2026; Zhejiang Univ. group of Fei Gao / Yanjun Cao): ≤6 cm cube with IR LEDs + IR camera + **UWB** + IMU; closed-form single-frame relative pose + multi-frame IMU-preintegrated refinement; robust to up to 90% bearing outliers; **RMSE 7.0 cm and 2.2 deg on real-world datasets**; number of robots and code not stated in abstract. - Multi-tag UWB relative localisation (arXiv 2107.08842): minimise ranging residuals across multiple UWB nodes per robot, fuse with odometry by particle filter; **0.312 m translation, 4.903 deg rotation** with three robots in a 6 m × 12 m area. — [search summary of ar5iv 2107.08842](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.08842)
- Lower-bound analysis of anchor-free UWB 6-DoF relative pose error vs node distance, altitude and tag geometry (arXiv 2309.15367). — [arXiv 2309.15367](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.15367)

### Inferences
- For option (A) on Starlings, there is no drop-in ROS 2 Jazzy package; the lab would be writing its own estimator. The best-documented design points to copy are Omni-swarm (4-DoF relative pose, UWB range + visual detection + loop closures, ~60 kB/s per drone) and DTVIRM-Swarm (sliding-window EKF + chi-square UWB gating + MDS-MAP init + covariance intersection).
- Yaw observability is the recurring hard part: DC-VIRO confirms global yaw stays unobservable for VIO+ranging to *estimated* anchors; practical systems resolve inter-drone yaw with one of (i) a common heading sensor (DTVIRM's magnetometer — fragile indoors), (ii) bearing/visual detection of the other drone (Omni-swarm, CREPES-X, 2407.01292), (iii) multiple UWB tags per vehicle so range differences encode heading (Fishberg; 2107.08842), or (iv) relative motion excitation over time (range-only + odometry, classic Zhou & Roumeliotis). Range-only with one tag per drone and no motion-diverse trajectories will not give yaw.
- The 59.4 kB/s per drone Omni-swarm figure (dominated by 54 kB/s keyframe descriptors) is a useful WiFi budget: a 5-drone swarm broadcasting at that rate is ~300 kB/s, trivial for WiFi but far above LinkTrack's in-band data link (see KQ6: 8–160 kbps).

### Gaps
- DTVIRM-Swarm full text (real/sim, drone count, numeric relative position and yaw errors, code) could not be retrieved: MDPI blocks automated fetches (HTTP 403). Needs a manual download of the open-access PDF.
- DC-VIRO per-robot numbers, anchor-calibration error, and NEES are only in figures; the "33 anchors" vs 3-anchor table conflict is unresolved.
- The ICRA 2025 paper "Overlapping free: Anchorless UWB-assisted relative pose estimation for multi-robot systems" (cited in CREPES-X's references) could not be located on arXiv in this pass.
- No independent reuse/replication of Omni-swarm or D²SLAM by other groups was found in this pass; GitHub issue counts suggest use, but no third-party papers were identified.
- Last-commit dates for Omni-swarm, D²SLAM, and mr-radio-localization were not visible on the fetched GitHub pages.

## KQ2. MRS UAV System estimator bank and in-flight switching (CTU Prague)

### Takeaway
MRS runs one estimator per localisation source in parallel, publishes a separate TF frame per estimator, and switches the controller's active estimator by service call or automatically on health failure; the switch is made seamless by "a virtual jump between the old and new coordinate frame" rather than by altering the physical setpoint. The ROS 2 port (`mrs_uav_managers` branch `ros2`) implements this as a `StateMachine::ESTIMATOR_SWITCHING_STATE` during which publishing is paused and the active estimator pointer is swapped.

### Cited Findings
- MBZIRC 2020 paper (arXiv 2012.05946): "state is estimated using three individual estimators: a GPS-based localization, an optic-flow odometry, and visual servoing relative to an observed brick … The optic-flow estimator is used as a backup in case the visual servoing fails." "The absolute position of the world frame depends on the actively used state estimator. When the state estimator is changed, the control pipeline synchronizes a virtual jump between the old and new coordinate frame, such that it is not noticeable to an outside observer." — [arXiv 2012.05946 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.05946)
- Same paper: "The process of adaptive and repeated switching from GNSS-based control into visual servoing and back is the main contribution of this part"; the switch to brick visual servoing happens once the UAV "successfully descends to the height of 0.8 m"; system ran ROS Melodic; code at github.com/ctu-mrs/mrs_uav_system and github.com/ctu-mrs/mbzirc_2020_wall_building. — [arXiv 2012.05946 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.05946)
- MRS UAV System paper (Baca et al., JINT 102:26, 2021; arXiv 2008.08050 v6 revised 23 Apr 2025): "multi-frame localization approach that fuses multiple sensors to estimate UAV states in different reference frames"; supports GNSS and GNSS-denied, outdoor-to-indoor transitions; "redundant estimators to back up unreliable localization sources"; used at MBZIRC 2017, MBZIRC 2020 and DARPA SubT with top placings; "years of applied research". No flight-hour count is given. — [arXiv 2008.08050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08050)
- Docs: EstimationManager "dynamically loads default [state estimator] or custom user-made plugins", publishes `uav_state` (velocities in `headed/frame_id`), `odom`, `height_agl`; "offers a service for switching the estimator used in control feedback"; it "checks the current estimator and switches to a healthy one if the current one becomes unhealthy. If none is healthy, it calls failsafe landing." TransformManager publishes TFs from `fcu` to `fcu_untilted_origin`, `world_origin`, `local_origin`, `stable_origin`, `fixed_origin`, `utm_origin`, `mapping_origin_tf`. Page refers to "the upcoming ROS2 version". — [ctu-mrs docs: managers](https://ctu-mrs.github.io/docs/features/managers/)
- GitHub discussion #183: estimation manager runs a separate estimator for each localisation source and moves between them based on estimator health metrics; configured via `custom_config.yaml`. — [ctu-mrs discussions #183](https://github.com/orgs/ctu-mrs/discussions/183)
- ROS 2 code (`mrs_uav_managers`, branch `ros2`, `src/estimation_manager/estimation_manager.cpp`, 1762 lines): service `~/change_estimator_in` (`mrs_msgs/srv/String`) → `callbackChangeEstimator`; refuses switching to `dummy`, `ground_truth`, `vins_kickoff` ("switching into these estimators during flight is dangerous with realhw"); requires `target_estimator->isRunning()`; sets `ESTIMATOR_SWITCHING_STATE`, calls `switchToEstimator`, then `changeToPreSwitchState()`. `switchToEstimator` does: `active_estimator_->setActive(false); active_estimator_ = target_estimator; active_estimator_->setActive(true); estimator_switch_count_++`. `switchToHealthyEstimator` iterates `estimator_list_` ("available estimators should be specified in decreasing priority order in config file") and picks the first `isRunning()`. If none, `timerCheckHealth` logs "Cannot switch to any healthy estimator. Triggering failsafe." and calls `~/failsafe_out` (`std_srvs/Trigger`). `timerPublish` does not publish while in `ESTIMATOR_SWITCHING_STATE`. Estimators are pluginlib plugins (`mrs_uav_managers::StateEstimator`) listed in the `state_estimators` parameter; `initial_state_estimator` must be one of them. — [estimation_manager.cpp (ros2 branch)](https://github.com/ctu-mrs/mrs_uav_managers/blob/ros2/src/estimation_manager/estimation_manager.cpp)
- `mrs_uav_managers` README (ros2 branch) just points to the docs page; branch name indicates ROS 2 but no distribution is named. — [GitHub mrs_uav_managers](https://github.com/ctu-mrs/mrs_uav_managers)

### Inferences
- The "no jump" mechanism is architectural, not a filter trick: each estimator owns its own origin frame; the controller's reference is re-expressed in the new estimator's frame at the switch instant (the "virtual jump"), so the commanded position in the *new* frame equals the current estimate in that frame and the vehicle does not move. The ROS 2 code confirms there is no state blending — it is a pointer swap with publishing paused for the switching tick.
- For the Starling swarm, the MRS pattern maps directly onto "own VIO frame" ⇄ "swarm-aligned frame" ⇄ "saved-map frame" as parallel estimators, each with its own TF origin, with a priority list and health gating; the swarm-alignment estimator would be just another plugin.
- Evidence of real flights is competition-grade (MBZIRC 2017/2020, DARPA SubT) but no quantified hours/flight count is published in the abstracts fetched.

### Gaps
- The exact "virtual jump" implementation (which component re-expresses the control reference; whether the tracker is re-initialised) was not located in the ROS 2 code fetched; only the estimator-side swap was read. The ControlManager side needs reading.
- Health-check criteria (innovation thresholds, timeouts) live in `timerCheckHealth` and estimator plugins; not extracted.
- ROS 2 distribution (Humble vs Jazzy) of the `ros2` branch not confirmed.

## KQ3. NVIDIA cuVSLAM / Isaac ROS Visual SLAM `localize_in_map`: requirements, reliability, Jetson/Jazzy support, known issues

### Takeaway
cuVSLAM can save landmarks + pose graph (`save_map`) and relocalise a fresh session into them given a position hint (`localize_in_map`, orientation hint ignored); NVIDIA publishes no relocalisation success-rate or accuracy figures, forum threads show frequent failures (CUVSLAM_LocalizeInExistDb errors 2/4, crashes), SLAM/localisation mode is single-stereo-camera only, and the Jazzy-based Isaac ROS stack only reached Jetson Orin with Isaac ROS 4.6 (Aug 2026, JetPack 7.2); Isaac ROS 5.0 (Sep 2026) moved to ROS 2 "Lyrical" and renamed the package `isaac_ros_cuvslam`.

### Cited Findings
- Concept page: "stored landmarks and the pose graph can optionally be saved using the ROS 2 SaveMap service"; saving is asynchronous and "can take some time". `LocalizeInMap` "requires a map file path and a prior pose" (initial guess); cuVSLAM "tries to match landmarks in the requested map to the current camera frames"; on success the map is loaded into memory, otherwise cuVSLAM keeps building a new map. Landmarks not found where expected are marked for deletion ("use cuVSLAM over a changing terrain"). IMU-only fallback acceptable for ~1 s, constant-velocity ~0.5 s. No accuracy numbers, no camera-identity requirement stated. — [cuVSLAM concept page](https://nvidia-isaac-ros.github.io/concepts/visual_slam/cuvslam/index.html)
- Tutorial: `/visual_slam/save_map` (`isaac_ros_visual_slam_interfaces/srv/FilePath`, field `file_path`); `/visual_slam/localize_in_map` (`srv/LocalizeInMap`, fields `map_folder_path`, `pose_hint`); the **orientation in `pose_hint` is currently ignored**; save target must be a new empty directory (contents overwritten); on failure "retry with a different valid pose_hint". No localisation radius, timing or success criteria given. Map format: a folder (binary contents undocumented). — [cuVSLAM Isaac Sim tutorial](https://nvidia-isaac-ros.github.io/concepts/visual_slam/cuvslam/tutorial_isaac_sim.html)
- Older API used actions `visual_slam/save_map` (`action/SaveMap`, `map_url`) and `visual_slam/load_map_and_localize`; service/action names differ across releases. — [forum: how to save_map](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/how-to-do-isaac-ros-visual-slam-save-map/339334); [forum: service cannot work](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/isaac-ros-visual-slam-ros2-service-cannot-work/339219)
- NVIDIA staff ("Raffaello", 4 Jun 2024): localisation-only is a parameter change (`enable_localization_n_mapping`); "Currently SLAM mode only works when using a single stereo camera." (31 Jul 2024): "the localization works even if the IMU fusion is disabled"; "If the localization is successful, cuVSLAM will load the map into memory. If not, it will proceed to build a new map." — [forum 295038](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/using-isaac-ros-vslam-questions-on-localization-mode-pose-graph-saving-offline-and-3d-reconstruction-with-nvblox/295038)

## KQ4. ORB-SLAM3 Atlas multi-map / map reuse: behaviour, relocalisation reliability, ROS 2 wrapper status

### Takeaway
ORB-SLAM3's Atlas keeps multiple maps, relocalises lost frames against *all* maps via a shared DBoW2 database, and merges them on revisit; the paper's multi-session EuRoC results demonstrate map reuse with 3.6 cm stereo-inertial accuracy, but save/load of the Atlas was community-patched (issue #128, 32 comments, crashes and camera-serialisation problems) and no independent study of cross-session relocalisation reliability under viewpoint/lighting change was found. The upstream repo is V1.0 (22 Dec 2021), GPLv3, ROS 1 Melodic only, 541 open issues; ROS 2 wrappers are third-party and Humble-centric with no confirmed Jazzy build.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract (arXiv 2007.11898 v2, 23 Apr 2021; T-RO DOI 10.1109/TRO.2021.3075644): "average accuracy of 3.6 cm on the EuRoC drone" (stereo-inertial) and 9 mm in TUM-VI room; multiple-map system with a new place-recognition method with improved recall; when tracking is lost "it starts a new map that will be seamlessly merged with previous maps when revisiting mapped areas"; "2 to 5 times more accurate than previous approaches". — [arXiv 2007.11898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898)
- Multi-session evaluation: EuRoC has 5 Machine Hall, 3 Vicon1 and 3 Vicon2 sessions; the authors "process sequentially all the sessions corresponding to each environment", each new session merging into the existing map; hard sequences V103/V203 handled thanks to the previous map. — [search summary of arXiv 2007.11898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.11898)
- Relocalisation mechanics: "When tracking is lost, the tracking thread tries to relocalize the current frame in all the Atlas' maps. If relocalized, tracking is resumed, switching the active map if needed"; a "unique DBoW2 database of keyframes … is used for relocalization, loop closing and map merging". Loading is via `System.LoadAtlasFromFile` in the settings YAML; if the file is missing a new Atlas is created silently. — [search summary incl. Mintlify docs](https://www.mintlify.com/UZ-SLAMLab/ORB_SLAM3/guides/map-save-load)
- Repo status: GPLv3 (commercial closed-source version by contacting authors); README header V1.0, 22 Dec 2021; 54 commits; 9.2k stars; 541 open issues; ROS examples "tested with ROS Melodic under Ubuntu 18.04"; no ROS 2 mention. — [GitHub ORB_SLAM3](https://github.com/UZ-SLAMLab/ORB_SLAM3)
- Issue #128 "how to use the SaveAtlas() and LoadAtlas() for relocalization?" (opened Sep 2020, closed 23 Dec 2021 by jdtardos, 32 comments): original Save/LoadAtlas "fail when used for relocalization"; bug in `MapPoint::PreSave`; save crashes if tracking was lost just before saving; load crashes during merge (`Sim3Solver.cc`, g2o `block_solver.hpp` assert); MapPoints missing after load; abstract `GeometricCamera`/Kannala-Brandt serialisation problems; IMU-mode localisation "still wrong" after load; PR #310 added Save/Load; stereo save/load works but "often fails to save again" after loading and continuing. **No one in the thread reports successful relocalisation after loading.** — [ORB_SLAM3 issue #128](https://github.com/UZ-SLAMLab/ORB_SLAM3/issues/128)
- Related open issues: #515 "Question about localization mode" (Mar 2022), #600 "localization mode accuracy with TUM dataset" (Jun 2022), #828 "Segmentation fault" (Nov 2023). — [ORB_SLAM3 issues search](https://github.com/UZ-SLAMLab/ORB_SLAM3/issues?q=is%3Aissue+relocalization+load+atlas)
- A real log from Stanford's UMI-on-Legs project shows a loaded atlas printing "Relocalization() failed." / "Fail to track local map!" then later "Relocalized!!" — transient failures in practice. — [Stanford slam_stdout log](https://real.stanford.edu/umi-on-legs/tossing/20240606_yihuai_tossing1/demos/demo_C3444250470334_2024.06.06_09.10.46.747067/slam_stdout.txt)
- ROS 2 wrappers (all third-party): `Mechazo11/ros2_orb_slam3` (Humble, last push 21 Nov 2025 — most recent found); `guyuehome/ORB_SLAM3_ROS2` (Humble, Ubuntu 22.04, adds stereo-inertial); `zang09/ORB_SLAM3_ROS2` (Foxy, mono/stereo/RGBD/stereo-inertial); `suchetanrs/ORB-SLAM3-ROS2-Docker` (Humble Docker); `curtesian/orb_slam3_ros_wrapper` (library-wrapper approach, "ongoing development"). **No repo confirmed to build on Jazzy**; only a Chinese-language guide says Ubuntu 24.04 works "with a few small modifications". — [search results](https://github.com/Mechazo11/ros2_orb_slam3); [guyuehome](https://github.com/guyuehome/ORB_SLAM3_ROS2); [zang09](https://github.com/zang09/ORB_SLAM3_ROS2)
- Multi-robot use of one ORB-SLAM map: CORB-SLAM (ORB-SLAM2-based collaborative system with map fusing/sharing, public code) is the closest precedent; a 2021 edge-server framework lets robots "register to an existing map, update it, or build new ones". Nothing found for ORB-SLAM3 specifically. — [CORB-SLAM](https://eudl.eu/pdf/10.1007/978-3-030-00916-8_45); [arXiv 2102.03228 summary](https://awesomepapers.io/robotics/papers/2102.03228)

### Inferences
- ORB-SLAM3's Atlas is designed for *in-process* multi-session merging; persisting it to disk and reloading on another robot is a bolt-on with a documented history of crashes. Any multi-drone "load one atlas on every drone" plan should budget for forking and hardening the serialisation (several forks exist: guyuehome, Mechazo11).
- Cross-camera reuse: ORB features + DBoW2 are camera-agnostic in principle, but the Atlas stores the camera model; loading a map built with one D435i's intrinsics into a session with different intrinsics is UNVERIFIED and likely to require identical settings files.
- Relocalisation reliability under viewpoint/lighting change is unquantified anywhere found; DBoW2 appearance-based place recognition is known to be lighting-sensitive (general knowledge, not a cited measurement).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or community numbers for ORB-SLAM3 relocalisation success rate across sessions or from novel viewpoints.
- Last-commit date for upstream ORB_SLAM3 not visible (README V1.0 Dec 2021; 54 commits suggests dormant).
- Jetson Orin NX runtime of ORB-SLAM3 stereo-inertial on D435i not found in this pass.

## KQ5. Single-drone VIO drift figures (OpenVINS, VINS-Fusion, cuVSLAM, ORB-SLAM3) for a drift simulator

### Takeaway
On EuRoC-class data, published position APE RMS values are roughly: ORB-SLAM3 stereo-inertial 0.03–0.04 m (3.6 cm paper average), OpenVINS 0.05–0.16 m, VINS-Fusion 0.07–0.24 m (sequence-dependent), with rotation APE RMS around 1–6 deg on the same sequences; cuVSLAM is only characterised by NVIDIA on KITTI (0.94% translation, 0.0019 deg/m). No source gives a yaw-drift-per-minute figure for any of these systems; percent-of-distance figures exist only for VINS-Mono (0.29%) and cuVSLAM (0.94%, KITTI).

### Cited Findings
- Sharafutdinov et al., "Comparison of modern open-source visual SLAM approaches" (arXiv 2108.01654 v2, Feb 2023), Table 5 "Results of SLAM algorithms tested on EUROC dataset", APE position max/RMS (m) and rotation max/RMS (deg), Intel i7-8565U: 
  - OpenVINS — V1_01: 0.11/0.05 m, 6.05/5.45 deg; MH_05: 0.48/0.16 m, 3.36/1.30 deg
  - VINS-Fusion — V1_01: 0.24/0.07 m, 10.19/5.60 deg; MH_05: 0.40/0.19 m, 4.81/1.87 deg
  - ORB-SLAM3 — V1_01: 0.07/0.03 m, 6.39/5.94 deg; MH_05: 0.11/0.04 m, 1.68/0.93 deg
  - Kimera — V1_01: 0.08/0.04 m, 6.34/5.59 deg; MH_05: 0.58/0.34 m, 3.82/2.34 deg
  - Basalt — V1_01: 0.05/0.03 m, 5.99/5.36 deg; MH_05: 0.17/0.09 m, 1.42/0.67 deg
  (Variant — mono/stereo, loop closure on/off — not specified in the table.) — [arXiv 2108.01654v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.01654v2)
- ORB-SLAM3 abstract: 3.6 cm average on EuRoC (stereo-inertial); 9 mm TUM-VI room; "2 to 5 times more accurate than previous approaches"; medians of 10 runs; stereo-inertial "three to four times more accurate than VINS-Fusion" (search summary of paper). — [arXiv 2007.11898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11898)
- GVINS-style GPS-VIO paper (arXiv 2203.02677) EuRoC ATE table: ORB-SLAM3 0.035 m average, VINS-Fusion 0.110 m average. — [arXiv 2203.02677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02677)
- UMS-VINS (arXiv 2303.08550) EuRoC RMSE: ORB-SLAM3 0.04 m vs VINS-Fusion 0.24 m on MH_01, ORB-SLAM3 lower on all ten sequences. — [arXiv 2303.08550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08550)
- Kimera2 (arXiv 2401.06323): "ORB-SLAM3 outperformed both Kimera and Vins-Fusion in most cases on EuRoC" (loop closure enabled). — [arXiv 2401.06323](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.06323)
- PO-MSCKF (arXiv 2407.01888) "RMSE in EuRoC datasets in meters": OpenVINS ≈0.11 m on MH01, VINS-Mono 0.15 m, OKVIS 0.33 m. — [arXiv 2407.01888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01888)
- OpenVINS robustness: a 2025 comparison reports OpenVINS succeeded on MH_01 and V1_01 but failed on several other EuRoC sequences (bad initialisation, motion blur). — [arXiv 2507.11241](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.11241)
- VINS-Mono real quadrotor flight: "The final drift is [0.08, 0.09, 0.13] m, resulting in 0.29% position drift" on a 61.97 m trajectory. — [arXiv 1708.03852](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03852)
- cuVSLAM: KITTI translation error 0.94%, rotation 0.0019 deg/m (= 1.9 deg per km), 0.007 s/frame on Jetson AGX Xavier; no EuRoC figures. — [isaac_ros_visual_slam index](https://nvidia-isaac-ros.github.io/repositories_and_packages/isaac_ros_visual_slam/index.html)
- Yaw drift: DIDO (arXiv 2203.03149) states learned/VIO odometry "still does not guarantee a perfect globally consistent yaw angle and position under long and highly maneuverable flight. The plausible solution is to provide an absolute observation, such as GPS or magnetic compass." No deg/min figure. — [arXiv 2203.03149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03149)

### Inferences
- The EuRoC rotation APE RMS values (≈5–6 deg on V1_01 for *every* system, including the most accurate) are dominated by the Vicon-room ground-truth alignment issue rather than estimator yaw drift, so they should not be used directly as yaw-drift rates; MH_05 (0.9–1.9 deg RMS over ~98 m / ~111 s) is a better yaw-drift proxy.
- For a drift simulator, defensible parameters are: translation random-walk giving ~0.3–1% of distance (VINS-Mono 0.29% flight; cuVSLAM 0.94% KITTI; EuRoC APE RMS/trajectory length for OpenVINS ≈0.16 m/98 m ≈0.16% on MH_05, VINS-Fusion ≈0.19%), and a yaw random walk on the order of 0.5–1.5 deg RMS per 100 m-class, 2-minute flight (MH_05 rotation RMS 0.93–1.87 deg across the four VIO systems). The per-minute yaw-drift rate itself is UNVERIFIED — no source quotes one.
- On a D435i (narrow FoV, rolling-shutter-free IR stereo, 30 Hz) results will be worse than EuRoC's global-shutter 20 Hz stereo with hardware-synced IMU; treat the above as optimistic.

### Gaps
- No published yaw drift in deg/min for OpenVINS, VINS-Fusion, cuVSLAM or ORB-SLAM3.
- No cuVSLAM EuRoC or indoor-drone drift figure from NVIDIA or third parties in this pass.
- No OpenVINS official EuRoC table was fetched (docs.openvins.com/eval-euroc.html returned 404); OpenVINS numbers come from third-party comparisons.
- No Jetson Orin NX runtime/accuracy for these systems with D435i.

## KQ6. UWB error figures: Nooploop LinkTrack and DW1000-class modules (noise sigma, per-pair bias, NLOS, update rate vs node count)

### Takeaway
Nooploop's own datasheet specifies 10 cm typical ranging accuracy with 5 cm standard deviation (1-D), 30 cm/15 cm in the 3rd dimension, and a hard trade-off in distributed-ranging mode (DR_MODE0) of 5 nodes @ up to 200 Hz, 10 @ 100 Hz, 20 @ 50 Hz, 50 @ 10 Hz. Fishberg & How measured LinkTrack P in practice: non-Gaussian, always-positive bias averaging 23 cm LOS rising to 64 cm when obstructed, per-antenna-pair biases from 1.8 cm to 27.7 cm (25.9 cm spread), and ~30 cm extra per wall; after per-pair calibration their 2D relative pose reached 0.09–0.29 m mean position and ~4–6 deg mean heading error.

### Cited Findings
**Nooploop LinkTrack datasheet V2.3 (Chinese, July 2023, Nooploop; hosted by AMOVLab)** — [LinkTrack Datasheet V2.3](https://wiki.amovlab.com/static/pdf/LinkTrack_Datasheet_V2.3_zh.pdf)
- Modes: LP (local positioning, roles tag/anchor/console), DR (distributed ranging, role node only), DT (data transfer). LP: "typical 1-D and 2-D positioning accuracy 10 cm, typical 3rd-dimension accuracy 30 cm; positioning frequency up to 200 Hz, up to 120 anchors, up to 200 tags". DR_MODE0: "typical ranging accuracy 10 cm, refresh rate up to 200 Hz, up to 50 nodes"; DR_MODE1: unlimited node capacity. DT: up to 3 Mbps, latency < 0.5 ms. (translated)
- Per-model spec table (all LTP/LTS variants): 1-D "10 cm @ accuracy, 5 cm @ standard deviation"; 2-D "10 cm @ accuracy, 5 cm @ R99"; 3-D "30 cm @ accuracy, 15 cm @ standard deviation". Footnote: accuracy "is a rough measurement obtained in actual scenarios with default shipping configuration; actual use must be tested in the specific environment". Onboard IMU (some batches): gyro noise 0.1 deg/s RMS, ±2000 deg/s; accel noise 300 µg, ±16 g; Euler roll/pitch 1 deg, "yaw: drifts". (translated)
- Headline capacity: "40 tags / 8 anchors / 1 console @ 50 Hz"; max communication distance 120 m @ LTP-AC, 500 m @ LTP-B; six RF bands from 3.5 to 6.5 GHz; TX gain adjustable 0–33.5 dB; power 1.1 W (LTP-AC) / 1.35 W (LTP-B). (translated)
- Table 12 "DR Mode parameters", DR_MODE0, NODE role — Capacity 5: UpdateRate options 1,2,5,10,25,50,100,200 Hz, DT rate 160000 bps, delay <5 ms; Capacity 10: up to 100 Hz, 80000 bps, <10 ms; Capacity 20: up to 50 Hz, 40000 bps, <20 ms; Capacity 50: up to 10 Hz, 8000 bps, <100 ms. DR_MODE1: unlimited capacity, 1–200 Hz, data rate "adjusting", delay = (1/UpdateRate)×1000 ms, at the cost of "reducing all nodes' output refresh rate and to some extent increasing ranging fluctuation" as neighbour density rises. Delay = 1/UpdateRate Max; DT Rate = UpdateRate Max × DT NoCache MaxLength. (translated)
- System channels (Table 9): CH0/1 centre 3494 MHz [3245–3744]; CH2/3 3994 MHz [3744–4243]; CH4/5 4493 MHz [4243–4742], all 499 MHz bandwidth; CH6 3994 MHz, 1331 MHz bandwidth.
- NLOS indicator: module outputs FP RSSI (first path) and RX RSSI at 0.5 dB resolution; "the difference between FP RSSI and RX RSSI can be used as a reference for judging line-of-sight vs non-line-of-sight". (translated)
- UWB chip: **not named in the datasheet text extracted** (DW1000 is widely assumed for LinkTrack P/S — UNVERIFIED from a primary source in this pass).

**Fishberg & How, "Multi-Agent Relative Pose Estimation with UWB and Constrained Communications" (arXiv 2203.11004, Mar 2022; IROS 2022)** — [arXiv 2203.11004 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.11004)
- Setup: Turtlebot2 + Jetson Nano + **four Nooploop LinkTrack P** per robot at R = 0.35 m radius (≈70 cm baseline, chosen to resemble a medium quadrotor), antennas upright, **DR mode, Channel 3 (3,744–4,243.2 MHz)**, Vicon ground truth. LinkTrack P chosen because "in our trials using 8 tags, the LinkTrack P provided measurements at steady 50Hz, while the Pozyx Developer Tag fluctuated around 5Hz"; 5.5×3×0.75 cm, 33 g, ROS driver out of the box.
- Noise: error between two stationary LOS nodes "appears neither zero mean nor Gaussian" (D'Agostino–Pearson p = 2.36e-6); sensors "consistently over-estimate"; mean error "approximately constant independent of distance". LOS: "average of 23 cm over-estimation"; obstructed by another antenna/bracket: "increases by 41 cm, to an average of 64 cm over-estimation". LinkTrack manual: signal passes 2–3 solid walls "but each wall introduces approximately 30 cm of error". RSSI gives no usable obstruction cue in their setup (fp/rx RSSI ≈ −81.0 / −78.0 dB constant). Antenna-pair "eclipse" spikes recur at specific relative headings.
- Calibrated per-pair biases µij (m), 16 pairs: 0.268, 0.266, 0.277, 0.230, 0.093, 0.112, 0.227, 0.188, 0.046, 0.018, 0.170, 0.078, 0.041, 0.065, 0.178, 0.095 — "as much as 25.9 cm between our observed best and worst pair". Calibration: robots 3 m apart, RB rotated ~60 deg/s, ~90 s.
- Yaw observability: 2D relative pose (x, y, heading) is recovered from the 16 inter-tag ranges of two 4-tag robots without exchanging odometry (multiple tags per agent give heading). Optimiser runs ~10 Hz on 50 Hz ranges, window W = 50.
- Results (proposed "Weighted"): mean position error 0.09 m (rot-cw/ccw), 0.20 m (traj-cw), 0.21 m (traj-ccw), 0.13 m (kidney-bean), 0.21 m (box), 0.29 m (both-move); mean absolute heading error 4.12, 4.43, 5.88, 5.84, 3.95, 4.22, 5.45 deg respectively (max heading error up to 104 deg in both-move during simultaneous fast counter-yawing). Raw (uncalibrated) ranges gave 0.38–0.98 m mean position and 9.8–16.8 deg heading. Box trial vs odometry-sharing baseline [19]: 0.21 m vs 0.25 m position, 4.22 vs 2.02 deg heading.

**Generic DW1000 figures**
- DW1000 double-sided two-way ranging on a ground vehicle: ranging interval ≈0.05 s, measurement standard deviation ≈2 cm (LOS). — [arXiv 2205.05939](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.05939)
- LOS accuracy "roughly ±10 cm" in a Dortmund study; Qorvo/Decawave specify DW1000 locates "to a precision of 10 cm". — [FH Dortmund](https://www.imsl.fh-dortmund.de/publications/SchmelterISR22.pdf); [Qorvo DW1000](https://store.qorvo.com/ProductDetail/DW1000-Qorvo/681945/)
- NLOS: partial blockage adds "a few decimetres", full blockage "up to a few metres" (TU Graz); classify-then-correct NLOS cut NLOS RMSE by up to 38% without hurting LOS (Stocker et al. 2021). — [TU Graz ranging note](https://www.tugraz.at/fileadmin/user_upload/Institute/ITI/Images/Aldin/centimetre-accurate_ranging.pdf); [Stocker 2021](https://www.tugraz.at/fileadmin/user_upload/Institute/ITI/cpsiot/Stocker_2021_UWB-NLOS.pdf)
- Crazyflie (DW1000 Loco deck): ranging biased by the antenna's doughnut radiation pattern; corrected EKF ≈20 cm RMS. — [Bitcraze UWB tag](https://www.bitcraze.io/tag/uwb/)

### Inferences
- Simulator parameters with sources: Gaussian core σ ≈ 2–5 cm (DW1000 2 cm LOS; Nooploop 5 cm spec), plus a constant positive per-pair bias drawn from ~[0.02, 0.28] m (Fishberg) that must be calibrated out (uncalibrated it degrades relative heading ~2–3×), plus NLOS/body-occlusion spikes of +0.3–0.4 m (airframe/antenna eclipse, per wall) to metres (full blockage). An explicit NLOS *outlier rate* per flight is not published anywhere found — UNVERIFIED; it is geometry-dependent (eclipse events recur at specific relative headings).
- Rate budget on LinkTrack DR_MODE0: a swarm of N drones with one tag each needs Capacity ≥ N: ≤5 nodes → 200 Hz, ≤10 → 100 Hz, ≤20 → 50 Hz, ≤50 → 10 Hz. Putting 4 tags per drone for yaw observability (Fishberg) means 4N nodes, so 5 drones = 20 nodes = 50 Hz max, 8 drones = 32 nodes → Capacity 50 → 10 Hz. Fishberg's 8-tag (2 robots) config ran steady 50 Hz, consistent with the table.
- In-band data (DT) is at most 160 kbps (5-node) down to 8 kbps (50-node) — insufficient for Omni-swarm-style 54 kB/s keyframe sharing; WiFi stays necessary for anything beyond range+ID+small odometry packets.

### Gaps
- Nooploop's UWB chip (DW1000 vs DW3000) not confirmed from the datasheet text; English product page not fetched.
- No measured LinkTrack ranging σ *in flight on a quadrotor* (propeller/airframe/altitude effects); Fishberg's are ground-robot measurements.
- No published NLOS outlier *rate* (fraction of measurements) for LinkTrack or DW1000 in a drone swarm.
