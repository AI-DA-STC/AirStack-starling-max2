## 2. The questions and the answers, in order

Corrections made during the discussion are marked **CORRECTION**; the corrected text is what stands.

### 2.1 What was 5.1? ("a trained Strike vs a trained Guard on Isaac Lab?")
No. Nothing was ever trained in Isaac Lab; Isaac Lab was only used to test (Test 6). Two different things came from NUS:
(1) NUS's **trained policies** (G19, S20, P3G, P3S), converted in Phase 3 (exact); (2) NUS's **training method** (the
recipe: Guard and Strike take turns learning against an archive of earlier opponents plus a scripted opponent; MAPPO).
5.1 tested whether STE's PyTorch rewrite of (2), trained from scratch in NUS's own 2-D game, produces policies like NUS's
code does. Owner then asked for this explanation in the docs: done (README §6, WHAT_WE_FOUND §6, commit `4422772d`).

### 2.2 The 5.1 rules and why FAIL; did the conversion of the recipe fail?
Rules (fixed 18 Nov before any result, `configs/nus_port.toml [gates.phase5_method_match]`): a every seed finishes; b
no two consecutive failed same-team generations in generations 1–6 (failed = the archived best's worst win ≤ 0.5
against its own training pool); c each entry's win rate against the other team's APF inside NUS's 12-entry range; d
cross-lineage strength vs NUS's, 95 % interval of the difference contains 0. Any failed part = FAIL; a FAIL is final
(3 more seeds only after an INCONCLUSIVE). Calibrated beforehand: passes NUS's own code on new seeds (C0), fails
untrained policies (N0). Reading: **not proven equal, not proven broken**. The port learns and is as strong as NUS's;
one seed fails by small margins; NUS's own seed 1 had one failed generation too (T38, 256 games).

### 2.3 Since 5.1 failed, no need to train in STE drone conditions?
No. Training in STE drone conditions is the goal (NUS's policies fail there). The FAIL only stops one route (NUS's recipe
via 5.2). The STE port is now STE's own code and still trains.

### 2.4 Is the STE Guard policy "my own strategy"? New hyperparameters, rules, conditions?
Same game (Test 5's game: NUS's hangar rules), STE conditions, STE's trainer: skrl IPPO, one PPO learner per Guard drone,
against S20 only, reward +1 hold / −1 reach (no shaping), STE's observation interface; two fixes from pilot P1 (untrained
Guards start standing still; one decision per 0.6 s, γ 0.99 per decision). The strategy (spread across the zone; the
filter acts like a wall) was learned, not designed.

### 2.5 Branching off from NUS's trained policies; what stays useful; simple trainer vs self-play
- Yes, branching from NUS's *trained policies* makes sense: their push-blocking does not survive the filter and 3-D.
  What stays useful from NUS: the game and scoring, S20 as an opponent, the APF baselines, and above all the self-play
  recipe.
- Learning algorithm (PPO / MAPPO / IPPO) ≠ self-play. Self-play = who you practise against. The stand-in trainer has none
  (fixed S20) — it overfits to its opponent (Lanctot 2017; our data: NUS specialists win 0.97–0.99 against their training
  opponent and 0.002–0.035 against another; G19 v S20 17/1000 in STE conditions). Plain self-play cycles and forgets;
  a pool of past opponents fixes it (fictitious self-play, Heinrich 2016; PSRO; AlphaStar league).
- **CORRECTION** (owner Q3, 20 Nov): the lead had listed the stand-in first; self-play with a pool is the
  recommendation. A Strike trained against the frozen STE Guard is only a cheap *probe*, not a method.

### 2.7 Team strategy, individual skills, robustness to losing a drone (owner Q2)
Review by the MARL role (20 Nov):
- NUS today: one `TeamActor` per team, the same parameters drive every drone (`runner/fixed_opponent_runner.py:192`,
  `jax/mpe.py:442-449`); each drone acts on its own row of observations, ordered self → teammates → opponents, holding all
  positions, no ID/mask/alive flag, width 9N+2 fixes the team size; no drop-out (games end only at the time limit; NUS
  said the method does not support drop-out, `PORT_PLAN.md:86`). APEX "specialists" are whole-team best responses used as a
  decaying action prior — not drone skills. Until NUS commit `3922803` (13 Jul 2026) the repo learned unsupervised team
  skills (MAPPO + DADS/METRA, shared skill latent); removed when NUS switched to area denial.
- STE stand-in: separate actor and critic per Guard drone; observation slots carry present flags
  (`core/observations.py:29-47`); drop-out mechanism exists (`core/game.py:169`) but `dropout_fraction = 0.0`.
- What it takes: shared actor + team critic + order-independent encoder over present-flagged lists (attention; MA-POCA,
  Cohen 2021 https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05992); random drone count and mid-game removal (RADAR, Phan AAAI 2021); optional
  skills + assigner that re-plans on loss (Yang et al. AAMAS 2020 https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03558; HCSP CoRL 2025
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04317). Owner decision needed: today's flight rule "any drop-out ends the game".
- "One shared brain per drone" (explained to the owner): one network copied onto every Guard drone; each drone feeds its
  own view and gets its own action, like players sharing one playbook; roles still differ by position.

### 2.9 Contact rules (owner's proposal) and the filter
Owner's proposal: CBF only within each team; Guards choose intercept / herding / kiting; validate in AirStack with
inter-team CBF off (drones crash on contact); on real drones keep CBF on all pairs, with a tag zone inside the CBF.
Review by the flight-interface and gatekeeper roles (20 Nov), with a desk run of the real `filter_velocities`:
- The filter **does not push**. Each tick it finds the smallest change to every drone's velocity command keeping every
  pair's centres ≥ 2r = 1.10 m apart (r 0.55 in `_ours` configs, alpha 2.5, 20 Hz, cap 1.0 m/s); the correction is split
  **evenly** between the two drones; an exempt ("fixed") drone is held and the other takes the full correction, but exempt
  applies to every pair of that drone. Desk run: Strike at 0.5 m/s into a still Guard → the pair moves off together at
  0.25 m/s; Guard pushing back at 0.33 → still driven back at 0.085 m/s; closest approach 1.100 m every case. The faster-
  commanded drone drags the pair; the Strike team is 1.5× faster (STANDARDS: Strike 0.5, Guard 0.33 m/s).
- Nothing gets inside 1.10 m except by tracking lag (0.03–0.10 m measured at radius 0.80, HTX-R2). A tag zone inside the
  filter distance would be decided by lag, not strategy.
- Training without inter-team CBF and flying with it on every pair repeats Test 4's failure (43 → 740): learned
  interceptions/herding get cancelled. Kiting beyond ~1.5 m is unaffected. The filter starts acting head-on at about
  1.32 m (0.5 m/s closing), 1.48 m (0.83), 1.57 m (1.0), plus ~0.125 m for 0.1–0.15 s response delay (derived).
- **Proposed instead (adopt with changes):** a proximity **tag at 3-D centre distance < 1.6 m** (filter distance + 0.5 m;
  capture radius as in reach-avoid games, Chen, Zhou, Tomlin 2017 https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.00450); both drones out and
  land, staying filter obstacles while descending; filter on every pair as flown. The owner's version stays a
  simulator-only research branch to measure the mismatch. Risk: a large radius favours a parked Guard — gate against it.
- The "filter as a wall" caveat: the STE Guard policy likely uses the Strike drones' own filter (they give way). That holds
  in a lab demo where one central filter runs all drones; a real attacker would not run our filter.
- **Filter "on every pair" explained**: one central filter in CMU's commander, every pair of drones (3v3 = 15 pairs:
  Guard–Guard, Strike–Strike, Guard–Strike), team-agnostic; exists because any collision indoors is a crash; central
  because the ground station has every drone's mocap position and sends every command. For the *game* it is wrong
  (attackers cooperate); for *safety* it is right.
- **Option B ("Guards take responsibility")**: for Guard–Strike pairs only, the Strike's command is left untouched and
  only the Guard swerves; Guard–Guard and Strike–Strike keep the 50/50 split. Safe if the Guard can escape; with the
  1.6 m tag a duel ends before 1.10 m, so option B is only a backstop (tracking error, landing descent); a fallback to
  50/50 when the Guard truly cannot escape is an emergency rule, not a game rule. Feasibility (lead read the code,
  551 lines): option A (team-mates only = skip inter-team pairs in `build_collision_constraints`) ~10 lines, sim only;
  option B needs per-pair "fixed" inside the solver, ~50–100 lines + tests, plus the same change in svg_training's
  filter copy. Lit.: Lyu, Luo, Dolan, CBFs for agents that do not cooperate symmetrically https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.09030;
  Wang, Ames, Egerstedt, safety barrier certificates; StringNet herding https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08925; Pliska et al.
  net capture https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13542.
- Two layers explained to the owner: strategy (policy decides where to fly: chase, block, hold) vs safety (filter, a
  seatbelt at 1.10 m). With the tag at 1.6 m, "intercept" = reach 1.6 m; the filter is almost never reached. Same rules in
  training, Isaac Lab, AirStack simulator and real flight.
- Operations: `~/land` lands every commanded drone (`swarm_commander.py:1612-1626`) — landing one pair needs a commander
  change; real 3v3 impossible (two drones) → real tests are 1v1, where the first tag lands everyone; AirStack sim
  "crashes" are PhysX hull bounces (convex hull + rotor colliders, no blade strikes, no downwash), not credible; PREFLIGHT
  forbids exemptions; real contact would need propeller guards and PREFLIGHT changes (not recommended).
- This overturns two FIXED rows in `STANDARDS.md` ("main game has no tag", "no tag radius"): needs the owner's ruling.

### 2.10 Deployment without onboard computers (owner, 20 Nov)
The Starlings have no Jetson; all commands go through the owner's mission-control laptop. Deployable now: the laptop runs
the shared network once per drone every 0.6 s, each with that drone's own view (well under 1 ms), and sends velocity
commands through CMU's commander and filter like any scenario. Using per-drone views keeps the door open to onboard later
(the Starling 2 Max has a ModalAI VOXL 2; whether it can run the network: not verified). Missing on the AirStack side: a
small game node that loads the policy file and publishes commands (an AirStack change, owner approval).

### 2.11 PX4 estimator (EKF2)
Why tier 2 has none: in simulation the true state is known; EKF2 is large C++; a CPU library called per drone, thousands of
drones × 200 Hz, would be slower than the AirStack simulator. The policy needs EKF2's *effect* (delay + smoothing + noise
on the state the controller sees). Order: (1) delay + noise model fitted to logs; (2) compare with the AirStack simulator
(real PX4 + real EKF2 on Pegasus) — owner asked "why can't I compare with the sim flight": yes, first, with the same
airframe settings on both sides to isolate the estimator; the sim cannot replace a real flight because of its SIM-ONLY
settings; (3) a real planned-push flight (NEAR-962) as the final timing check; (4) a simplified batched PyTorch EKF only
if (1)–(3) show the delay model is not enough. Also add a drag term (Pegasus has a simple drag; tier 2 none).

### 2.16 Real flight on Starlings, Crazyflies or a mix (owner, 20 Nov)
The owner asked whether the real flight could be tested on the two Starlings and on Crazyflies too, since the lab has many
Crazyflies and only two Starlings, and then named three fleets: all Starlings, all Crazyflies, or a mix in one game. The
lab's Crazyflie work is in a separate repository (`~/CrazySwarm2-with-Mocap_Strike-and-Guard`: Crazyswarm2 and OptiTrack,
not AirStack; read-only for this folder). Three specialists read both repositories (architecture and flight interface,
drone dynamics, learning and planning) and the advisor reviewed the result. Nothing was flown or launched; every number
below is from reading code and documents or from the Crazyflie repository's offline planner, and "estimate" marks the rest.

**What was found.**
- The parent design already puts real flight as "Crazyflies first, then Starlings" (`docs/DESIGN.md:50`,
  `docs/THE_GAME.md:42`); this track's L8 had kept only the Starling 1v1.
- The Crazyflie repository's Guard demo flies N defenders against M attackers with both strategies chosen by name, a
  strategy can be loaded from an outside module, and every command passes a safety layer built on AirStack's CBF solver
  copied byte for byte. It flew 4 v 3 on 6 Nov 2026. A trained policy would enter as an adapter (observation builder,
  decision hold, action scaling; about 150 lines, estimate) with no edit to that package. A stand-in policy was run
  through the offline planner; no real policy exists, so real inference is not tested.
- Two Starlings cannot show the programme's sizes (2v1 needs three drones, 5v3 eight) or play after a tag: AirStack's
  land command lands every drone. The Crazyflie engine lands tagged drones one at a time.
- The tag rule and filter option B (P2) are a rule plug-in and about 20–30 lines on the Crazyflie side (estimate),
  against a change in CMU's code on the AirStack side. Option B stays the owner's ruling (P2); cheaper hardware does not
  move that gate.
- A Crazyflie result says nothing about the Starling's response, PX4, EKF2 or AirStack. It is reported as "the game on
  Crazyflies", never as Starling evidence.
- Shrinking the game to Crazyflie size is not flyable (it falls under that repository's own separation floors) and would
  be a second training programme. The sound first route is **the Starling-sized game, in metres, on Crazyflies**: the
  same 1.10 m filter distance, speeds and zone.

**Offline check of that route** (Crazyflie planner, 1.10 m sphere, speeds 0.33 / 0.5 m/s, lag 0.6 s, height band
0.5–1.8 m, 100 simulated matches per case, the repository's built-in strategies and a stand-in):

| Case | Solver emergencies |
|---|---|
| 1v1 and 2v1, arena no larger than a 6.5 m square | 0 |
| 3v3 and 5v3, the same arena | in 5 of 100 matches each: fails the planner's safety verdict |
| 5v3, the same arena, lag 0.15 s | in 3 of 100 matches: the cause is the wide distance in a tight arena, not lag |
| 3v3 and 5v3, the Crazyflie room's own size (about 10 × 10 m) | 0 in 120 matches |

Eight drones at the Starling's filter distance may therefore not fit the 6.5 m lab arena on any platform; this goes to
gate G0 with team sizes (P9). The planner's own start marks are refused at this distance; the runs used hand-set marks.

**Open, and decided only by measurement: one policy file for both drones, or one per drone type.** A Crazyflie in the
logged flights trails its setpoint by about 0.56 s (default PID controller; the repository's own design notes), where
the Starling's fitted response is about 0.15 s late (exploratory fit). If the Starling-trained policy tolerates the
Crazyflie's response, one file serves both; if not, Crazyflies need their own tier 1 drone model (25–40 h, estimate) and
their own training run. Parent STANDARDS §5 rules 4–5 (a named fitted set per policy, narrow randomisation) speak against
covering both with wide randomisation.

**The mix.** A 0.557 kg Starling above a 30 g Crazyflie is an unmeasured downwash case (estimate: the Crazyflie cannot hold
height in the Starling's column), and a contact destroys the Crazyflie. Each flight stack filters only its own drones, so a
Starling–Crazyflie pair has no filter between them today; the lowest-risk fix is that each stack treats the other's drones
as fixed obstacles. Training would need two drone models in one game and a drone-type input, and the evaluation gains a
fleet axis (about five times the evaluation work, estimate). The one mix worth doing later is Starling Guards against
Crazyflie Strikes: each team stays one drone type.

**Facts from the owner (20 Nov).** Both fleets fly in the same room on the same OptiTrack rig, at different Motive rates
(the Starling setup runs 240 Hz, the Crazyflie pipeline is tuned to 50 Hz). The Crazyflie flight logs of 6 Nov 2026 are
not on the training workstation, so a response fit from them waits until they are brought over.

**Lead's recommendation (PROPOSED, the owner has not ruled).** All-Crazyflie first on hardware, at Starling metres; the
Starling 1v1 as planned; the mix deferred until both have flown. Cost, estimates: 35–50 h on the Crazyflie side, built by

Proposed first step: a zero-GPU task in phase L0, "Crazyflie route check" (does the STE Guard policy survive a
Crazyflie-like response in tier 1; arena fit per team size; the adapter and rule plug-in specification), and a fleet

## 7. Findings of L0.12 for gate G0 (L012-G0, 21 Nov 2026)

From record T46 (INFO) and T47 (ABORTED), observed in the training simulator (tier 1, 3v3, T44's 1,000 starts); not a
